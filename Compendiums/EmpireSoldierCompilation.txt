# Empire Soldier Compilation
This document contains a compilation of divine player options, sacred magic items, and a collection of NPC holy warriors who fanatically fight for their faith. These NPCs are usually human, but many of them could be members of other races serving different religions. Regardless of their origin or specific faith, they all share a zealous and militant devotion to their religious order that could push them to acts of heroism or villainy, depending on the needs of your adventure.

## Magic Armaments of The Empire

The armories of mighty human empires often hold magical implements of war reserved for lords and heroes of great standing. Some are ancient artifacts from the empire's founding; others are newly forged armaments imbued with eldritch enchantments or divine blessings. Adventurers who perform great service may occasionally earn such items as rewards, or pry them from enemies who looted them from the Empire's armories and fallen heroes.

#### Amber Bow
*Weapon (Longbow), Very Rare (Requires Attunement), 7,000 gp*

*Carved from the oak of the most ancient forests, this bow was crafted by order of the Amber Druids to slay the unnatural behemoths plaguing their land. It conjures a luminous arrow that glows a faint honey-gold, and when loosed, that golden arrow can fell even the mightiest monstrosities.*

You gain a +2 bonus to attack rolls made with this weapon.

When you take the Attack action on your turn while holding this weapon, you can forgo all your attacks to cast *Amber Spear** (save DC  10 + your Dexterity modifier + the number of attacks forgone). Increase the spell's effective level by 1 for each attack forgone. If only one creature makes a saving throw against the spell and it is a Beast, Monstrosity, Giant, Dragon, or Fey that is Huge or larger, the damage is doubled.

The bow can't be used this way again until the next dawn.

#### Halberd of Power
*Weapon (Halberd), Very Rare (Requires Attunement), 8,000 gp*

*This polearm was forged from the iron of a meteorite that fell during the celebration of the Empire's founding. Its wielders are said to draw might from the heavenly metal, striking with otherworldly strength.*

While holding this weapon, you know the Overwhelming Attack* and Pushing Attack maneuvers from the Battle Master subclass of the Fighter class. The saving throw DC equals 8 + your Proficiency Bonus + your Strength modifier.


The weapon has five Superiority Dice, each a d10. These dice are added to any Superiority Dice you have from another source while you are holding the weapon, and they are used to fuel your maneuvers. A Superiority Die is expended when you use it.

The weapon regains 1d4 + 1 expended Superiority Dice daily at dawn.

While holding the Halberd, you gain a +1 bonus to the saving throw DCs of your maneuvers. 

**New maneuver described at end of section*

#### Hammer of War
*Weapon (Warhammer), Legendary (Requires Attunement), 50,000 gp*

*Said to be one of the most powerful weapons ever forged, gifted to the mightiest hero of the age. In the hands of a true champion, there are no challenges that cannot be overcome.*

You gain a +3 bonus to attack and damage rolls made with this weapon, which deals Force damage instead of Bludgeoning damage. It deals an extra 2d8 Force damage to any target it hits, or 2d10 if you use both hands to wield it. When you miss with this weapon, you hit instead, except you deal only half damage and the creature suffers no effects other than damage from the attack.

When you score a critical hit with this weapon, you can roll three additional weapon damage dice when determining the extra damage for the critical hit.

#### Helm of the Indomitable
*Helmet, Rare (Requires Attunement), 5,000 gp*

*Decorated with an ornate golden griffon that sits atop the cap with its left talon outstretched. Commanders who don the chapeau have their tenacity magnified to the point where they shrug off death-inducing blows.*

You gain one use of the Indomitable feature from the Fighter class, even if you don't have the feature. You regain this expended use when you finish a Long Rest.

When you use your Indomitable feature or the Shared Resilience feature of the Banneret subclass of the Fighter class, if the effect the creature is making the saving throw against allows a creature to take only half damage on a successful save, the creature instead takes no damage on a successful save and only half damage on a failed save. Additionally, if damage from the effect would reduce a creature to 0 hit points, it drops to 1 hit point instead.

#### Rune Fang
*Weapon (Longsword), Very Rare (Requires Attunement), 24,000 gp*

*It is said that only twelve of these blades were ever made by one of the greatest dwarf artificers to ever live, and given as gifts to a band of chiefs who united their tribes in solidarity with the mighty dwarven strongholds to push back the advancing hordes that sought to bring down the bastions of these brave folk. Since that time, a number of those tribes have grown into dukedoms and provinces, their lords wielding the etched weapons as a sign of office. Others have been lost as the leaders and their lands were annihilated by invading armies. Such blades are priceless, for no other soul has been able to replicate such masterpieces. The blades are as light as air and able to cut through the thickest steel plate and hardest dragon hide as if they were little more than sheets of paper.*

When you miss with this weapon, you hit instead, except you deal only half damage and the creature suffers no effects other than damage from the attack.


**New maneuver described at end of document*

#### Shield of Protection
*Armor (Shield), Rare (Requires Attunement), 1,500 gp*

*Commissioned by a timorous count for his personal guardian, the shield is able to provide even greater protection to those next to it than to the wielder.*

Whenever you use the Protection Fighting Style to protect a creature while holding this shield, you can add 3 to the target's AC until the end of the current turn.

\columnbreak

#### Silver Horn
*Wondrous Item (Instrument), Uncommon (Requires Attunement by a Fighter), 1,000 gp*

*Used by commanders to turn the tide of countless battles. When blown, the conviction of the warrior playing the instrument becomes manifest as a roar unlike any other. Allies who hear it, even those who are maimed and beaten, rise to fight with renewed vigor.*

When you use your Second Wind feature, you can blow this horn. If you do, you roll 2d10 instead of 1d10, and you can choose any number of Bloodied creatures within 60 feet of you (which can include you). You can divide the Hit Points you would gain from this feature among them. This feature can't restore a creature to more than half its Hit Point maximum.

#### Spear of the Fates
*Weapon (Spear), Uncommon (Requires Attunement), 1,000 gp*

*Said to have been fashioned by a mad seer of the fanatical Inquisition whose mind was eroded by thousands of visions of crimes and wrongdoings yet to take place, stretching even to the end of time, which were imprinted onto the weapon. The spear holds that manic foresight within its tip. When pointed at a soul, the spear will hum and shake, recognizing the sins yet to take place and seeking to prevent such events from unfolding.*

At the start of your turn, you can choose to predict the fate of up to three creatures you are aware of within 60 feet of you. When you predict a creature's fate, roll a d6. On a roll of 5 or 6, the creature is marked by the weapon until you next use this property to mark another creature. While marked, whenever you hit the creature with the weapon, it takes an additional 1d8 Piercing damage. If the creature's hit points are 20 or fewer after taking this damage, it is reduced to 0 hit points.

Once you mark a creature, you can't mark that creature again for the next 24 hours. A creature immune to Divination spells can't have its fate predicted.

#### Laurels of Victory
*Wondrous Item, Very Rare (Requires Attunement), 12,000 gp*

*These enchanted medals of honor are woven with magic by Imperial Mages and are bestowed by the Emperor upon heroes who have proven their devotion to their lord through their valor. Such a symbol amplifies the presence of the warrior who wears it. Adversaries who look upon such a figure are filled with doubt and are likely to cower and flee should the odds of their victory even be slightly against them.*

You learn the Commanding Presence and Menacing Attack maneuvers from the Battle Master subclass of the Fighter class. The saving throw DC equals 8 + your Proficiency Bonus + your Strength or Dexterity modifier (your choice).

The weapon has five, each a d10. These dice are added to any Superiority Dice you have from another source, and they are used to fuel your maneuvers. A Superiority Die is expended when you use it.

The weapon regains 1d4 + 1 expended Superiority Dice daily at dawn.

Additionally, a creature has disadvantage on saving throws against your Battle Master maneuvers if you have hit it at least twice this turn with a weapon attack.

#### Marvellous Magnifier
*Wondrous Item, Very Rare (Requires Attunement), 12,000 gp*

*Devised by a fastidious tinker, when one looks through this small monocle and focuses, they are able to see with clarity the most minuscule detail. It was said that no flaw was able to avoid the creator's inspection while wearing such a glass, nor could any target escape his rifle.*

You learn the Survey* and Precision Attack maneuvers from the Battle Master subclass of the Fighter class.

Once per turn, when you use a maneuver, you can roll 1d10 and use the number rolled instead of expending a Superiority Die. You can use this feature only if you haven't moved during this turn, and after you use it, your Speed is 0 until the end of the current turn.

**New maneuver described at end of section*

#### Pearl Dagger
*Weapon (Dagger), Very Rare (Requires Attunement), 8,000 gp*

*Such gleaming and flawless blades are light as a feather. The wielder is able to invoke a fleetness that allows them to strike with deadly speed.*

While you are holding this weapon, you know the Feinting Attack and Lunging Attack maneuvers from the Battle Master subclass of the Fighter class. The saving throw DC equals 8 + your Proficiency Bonus + your Dexterity modifier.

The weapon has five Superiority Dice, each a d10. These dice are added to any Superiority Dice you have from another source while you are holding the weapon, and they are used to fuel your maneuvers. A Superiority Die is expended when you use it.

The weapon regains 1d4 + 1 expended Superiority Dice daily at dawn.

Finally, when you roll a Superiority Die while holding this weapon, you can reroll it, but you must use the new roll.

\columnbreak

#### Repelling Armor
*Armor (Half Plate), Very Rare (Requires Attunement), 6,500 gp*

*This gilded suit of armor was fashioned as thanks to the empires of man after the great alliance against the forces of Chaos. A potent barrier of translucent force eddies around the wearer, halting all manner of weapons that would strike them.*

Whenever a creature attempts to make a melee attack against you for the first time on a turn, the attacker must make a DC 15 Strength saving throw. On a failed save, it can't attack you with melee attacks until the end of the turn, and it must choose a new target for its attack or the attack is wasted. On a successful save, it can attack you on this turn.

#### Twice-Blessed Armor
*Armor (Plate), Very Rare (Requires Attunement), 7,000 gp*

*Etched with sacred prayers by two great Grand Theogonists. Those in the burnished shell who are bestowed with its boons will share such mystic bounties with their comrades.*

When another allied creature you can see within 30 feet of you casts a spell of 4th level or lower, such as *Bless* or *Fly*, you can allow the creature to increase the spell's effective level by 1. If it does, you must be one of the spell's targets, or the spell dissipates with no effect and the action, Bonus Action, or Reaction used to cast it is wasted.

### New Battle Master Maneuvers

#### Overwhelming Attack
When you hit a creature with an attack roll using a weapon or an Unarmed Strike, you can expend one Superiority Die to attempt to overwhelm the target. The target must succeed on a Strength saving throw or take damage equal to three times the number rolled on the Superiority Die. The damage is the same type dealt by the original attack.

#### Survey
As a Bonus Action, you can expend one Superiority Die to take the Search action. Roll the die and add the number rolled to the ability check.

### New Spell
#### Amber Spear

*3rd-level evocation*
___
- **Casting Time:** 1 action
- **Range:** 60 feet
- **Components:**  V S M (A Piece of Amber)
- **Duration:**  Instantaneous
- **Classes:**   Druid, Ranger
___

You conjure a large amber hunting spear and launch it in a straight line at a point you can see within range. Upon reaching its destination or striking a solid surface, the spear shatters, producing an explosion within a 10-foot-radius sphere centered on that point. The spear can pass through spaces occupied by creatures and objects of large size or smaller. Each creature the spear moves through, as well as those within the explosion's radius, must make a Dexterity saving throw. On a failed save, a creature takes 6d6 piercing damage; on a successful one, the damage is halved.

***At Higher Levels.***  When casting this spell using a 4th-level spell slot or higher, the range increases by 20 feet and the damage by 1d6 for each slot level above 3rd.

# Soldiers

## Archers
*Classic Ranged Soldiers & Marksmen*<br>
**Habitat:** Urban; **Treasure:** Armaments <br>
Archers are among the most common type of soldier, with regiments observing the heavens as volleys of arrows streak toward the earth, raining down upon enemy formations.

### Deathjacks
Though woodland paths and city alleyways may conceal shadowed figures that stride noiselessly through the landscape, few ever notice their presence until arrows spring from the darkness and lodge themselves into the unsuspecting victims.

Such masterful feats are performed by archers known as Deathjacks. These cunning marksmen serve as scouts and assassins for armies, able to approach enemy encampments and regiments without a soul catching wind of their presence. They can even slink into the heart of hostile territory to strike vulnerable targets, then vanish without consequence as they silently retreat, leaving chaos in their wake. As fallen soldiers' comrades search bewilderedly for their murderers, those responsible are already long gone.

#### DeathJacks Tactics
**Target the least perceptive creature.** The Deathjacks should attack the enemy they believe is least likely to notice them, reducing the chance of being discovered.

**Avoid prolonged engagements.** If they are discovered, the Deathjacks should disengage.

### Order of the Bow
Some archers view their craft as more than merely the mastery of a deadly weapon. To them, archery is a meditative experience, a pursuit of tranquility in which there is no greater calm than drawing one's bowstring. As the string is pulled taut, the mind becomes free of all burdens, leaving only the archer and their mark. When the arrow is finally loosed, it flies straight and true.

Those who embrace this meditative philosophy often form guilds and monasteries where they hone their techniques alongside like-minded warriors. These organizations are commonly known as the Order of the Bow.

Such sages are forces to be reckoned with. Their mastery allows them to launch projectiles with such precision that even the protections of their enemies offer little refuge. They can pin foes from leagues away, and even those shrouded by foliage, rubble, or debris find little safety from their watchful eyes.

#### Order of the Bow Tactics
**Force enemies out of cover.** When positioned safely behind cover, the archer can pressure enemies into advancing toward them. Enemies may struggle to find a safe position from which to retaliate.

**Pair with melee units.** This archer works particularly well alongside melee units. As enemies advance to close the distance, melee allies can intercept them.

**Maintain a 30-foot distance.** If enemies manage to close in, the archer should prioritize maintaining roughly 30 feet of distance.

**Let melee units protect them.** If an enemy continues closing the gap, melee allies should engage them before they can reach the archers, allowing the archer to continue firing from a safer position.

When the amphibious marauders grow numerous enough to weather the barrage, any beast fortunate enough to haul itself free of the water finds its entrance greeted by a steel-toed boot crashing against its unctuous form, sending it hurtling back into the depths, usually with a fresh iron-tipped bolt not far behind.

### Crossbowmen Bulwark
Among the most dangerous warriors to bear bolt-launching implements are those who use the weapon in tandem with massive, towering shields known as pavises: sheets of wood adhered together, covered in leather and canvas, reaching nearly the height of the wielder and covering the whole of the user's body. The warrior is able to move forward as enemy fire falls in vain, arrows and enemy bolts sticking into the handheld wall. Once close enough to the desired quarry, the marksman drives the shield into the ground, a great spike at the bottom being driven deep into the earth, turning the pavise into a full-fledged barricade. From behind the cover of this makeshift rampart, these warriors draw forth and load massive crossbows.

\columnbreak

They only peer out from above the crest, using the shield to steady the massive contraption as they take aim and smite oncoming combatants. The men who implement such tactics are known as Bulwarks and have changed the course of wars; many mercenary groups and armies possess them, forever carving their names into history.

#### Bulwark Tactics
Bulwarks work well alongside other melee fighters to keep foes occupied. They try to hold their position, forcing enemies to close the distance. If an enemy reaches melee range, the Bulwark uses the Dodge action to retreat. When multiple Bulwarks fight together, they should scatter so opponents must chase one while the others fire from range.

The mightiest of soldiers grip swords nearly the length of a full-grown man, with massive blades of steel. ### Crimson Greatswordsmen
The Crimson Greatswordsmen are a rare breed that possess a particularly potent lust for violence. They have gained their grisly title from the fact that once their blades cut deep, crimson coats both the swordsman and his adversaries. As fever seizes the gore-caked soldier, they sweep their massive blade with ever-growing power, and a vehemence courses through every fiber of their being, allowing body and mind to withstand the most brutal punishments.

#### Crimson Greatswordsmen Tactics

Crimson Greatswordsmen should prioritize the lowest-AC target to trigger Bathed in Blood.

## Halberdiers
*Grim Soldiers Wielding Heavy Polearms*<br>
**Habitat:** Urban; **Treasure:** Armaments <br>

Halberdiers are soldiers armed with <br> axe-like polearms. They are generally<br> used to guard other troops against cavalry<br> and heavily armored opponents.

### Halberdier Mariners

Many river boats run the breadth of the land, <br> transporting people and vital goods along their <br> treacherous waterways. Such vital lifelines are <br> prime targets for any who would wish to cripple <br> the towns and cities along their banks. These <br> rivers are also home to all manner of lurking <br> aquatic nightmares, whose appetites may cause <br> such creatures to see these vessels as nothing <br> more than floating banquets.

To repel such raiders, many halberdier mariners <br> are hired to join their river-bound crews. A boisterous <br> lot, who, despite their uncouth bearing, are potent warriors when violence rears its head. Adept at fighting atop decks or even amid the rapids of rivers, they strike out against any pirate or beast that would attempt to board their barges.
Such men-at-arms are said to possess robust courage, fueled by a steady stream of strong brew. These intoxicated ruffians will charge fierce and true against whatever rises from the depths.
 
  
#### Halberdier Mariner Tactics        
Mariners will take a swig when they start combat. They will also take a swig whenever they lose the Emboldened condition, or if they are attacking a target against which they would already have advantage.

### Halberdier Virtuosos
Novices in the trade of battle look upon halberds as just axes with extended reach, failing to see the vast variety of death such versatile weapons may deliver to their opponents when guided by the hands of a true artisan of combat. The hooked blade may be sent swiftly around a passing cavalryman's armor-covered legs, and with a pull, pluck the rider from their saddle. The crowning steel spike atop the halberd is pointed forward and thrust with precision, sliding between steel plates to strike with little opposition and sink deep.

Blunt knobs of metal on the back of the polearm come crashing down like hail into helmets and chest plates, causing such protective shells to reverberate and bash against their wearers with such force that they leave them gasping and disoriented.

A true master’s use of the weapon has become so ingrained that each of these maneuvers is second nature. A slight rotation of the halberd is made, and the weapon is lunged back toward the target, with deadly protrusions hurtling forward, ready to bash, tear, or puncture.

### Silver Bullets

There are evils that enter the realm of men at the witching hour with only the sickly light of the moon to outline their misshapen forms, drawn to the warmth of living to feed their endless hunger. Such demons, spirits, and unholy beasts rampage across the gloom-shrouded realm without fear of any repercussion, for these otherworldly nightmares are not so easily purged from the mortal world. Even the most injurious blows do little to slow such monsters as they continue to shamble or drift forward, undeterred.

However, such foul resilience languishes when these creatures are struck by the hallowed weaponry forged by those who have devoted themselves to the eradication of the unnatural. Few of these blessed weapons are able to bring as much ruin to these dreadful spawn as a silver bullet fired from a rifle by one whose heart is seeded with riotous fury.

Whereas a normal piece of ammunition may wedge itself in a horrid creature’s mass of rotting flesh with minimal or no effect (or, in the case of an apparition, pass through its vaporous form ineffectively), such holy ordnance is not so easily neglected by the wicked. Ghouls and zombies scream as they are released from their painful existence, white embers cleansing their defiled flesh while their scorched limbs crumble away like ashen twigs in a bonfire. Spectres and wraiths howl with awe and terror as the gleaming bullet floats suspended within their ethereal figures, lighting them ablaze as if the spirits were made of lantern oil.

<br>


> ##### New Condition: Burning
> The creature has been set ablaze by something that continues to damage it each round until it is extinguished. The default source described here is fire, but other sources could deal different types of damage, such as acid.
> * **Default Damage:** A burning creature takes 1d4 fire damage at the end of each of its turns unless the source that inflicted the condition states otherwise.
> * **Ending the Condition:** As an action, the burning creature can extinguish the fire by dropping prone and rolling on the ground. The fire also goes out if it is doused, submerged, or suffocated. For example, another creature could use an action to put out the flames with their cloak or by pouring water on them.
> * **Multiple Sources of Burning:** If a burning creature would gain this condition again from another source, the new burning effect only replaces the old if it deals more damage each round. Otherwise, it is ignored.

### Surefires
There are eldritch woodlands where the rays of the sun are warded away by a canopy woven of twisted branches. No path can be carved, for any trail is ardently reclaimed by the surrounding wilderness, whipped away by a tide of foliage. In this primeval domain, it is the beastmen that stalk beneath the murky canopy and rule with impunity. Few travellers are able to enter such abomination-infested undergrowth without being dragged into the shadows and torn to pieces.

Riflemen known as the Surefires are among the few who are able to contest this ground through the application of masterful training and firearms of the highest quality. Whereas most marksmen, wading through the thicket, make easy prey for hungering savages crouched among the brush, only to spring forth and rob the unsuspecting soldier of any edge his weapon might bestow, Surefires are not cut down by such ambushes. 

Close quarters do not dampen these riflemen’s deadly talents. They are able to sidestep such bloodthirsty charges, press the muzzle of their firearm against the slavering jaw or barrel chest of their primitive attacker, with a pull of a trigger. The blast sends their tactless assailant hurtling backward reduced to a heap of scattered, viscous chunks scattered among the vegetation.



#### Surefire’s Tactics
Surefires choose one of the following three tactics based on the situation:
* **Safe Range:** When enemies cannot reach them, the Surefire fires and uses its bonus action to quickly reload, maximizing damage output each turn from a safe distance.
* **Closing Enemies:** When opponents are approaching, or about to close the distance, the Surefire takes whatever cover is available to hide, then attacks with advantage for extra damage.
* **Cornered:** When an opponent has it cornered with no place to hide, the Surefire uses Barrel to the Face to gain advantage followed by Rapid Reload.

## Spearmen
*Loyal Guards & Foot Soldiers*<br>
**Habitat:** Urban; **Treasure:** Armaments <br>
Spearmen are among the most common soldiers found standing watch over villages and making up the bulk of an imperial army. They grip a polearm in one hand and a shield in the other.

### Spearmen Sentinels
Those spearmen most skilled at keeping watch and intercepting foes who try to bypass them and strike at other vulnerable targets are known as Sentinels. Many serve as security for nobles and other figures of high standing. In war they are charged with ensuring that artillery crews or spellcasters are not disrupted by enemy forces.

### Spearmen Valorguard
There are few regiments of foot troops that pose a greater threat to cavalry than the Valorguard. These specialized warriors have been trained to brace against the most powerful charging opponents and break their momentum. Greater still is the iron will of such masters: those behind their barricade of steel and determination are filled with courage and will not falter even as the most hideous of foes barrel toward them.

It is said that when a great invasion of beastmen emerged from the deep woods to consume the city of Soithune, it was a group of these warriors who formed a living wall between the rest of Soithune’s army and the herd of minotaurs at the head of the savage force.

## Swordsmen
*Frontline Soldiers & Warrior Duelists*<br>
**Habitat:** Urban; **Treasure:** Armaments <br>
Swordsmen go into battle with blade and shield in hand. Such warriors hold the front lines effectively, keeping enemy opponents at bay while polearms and arrows finish off those locked in place.

### Militants
Militants stride forth to bring about divine justice. These men have pledged themselves to a god or holy mission and serve with fervent devotion. Though they possess no divine magic like paladins or clerics, their faith gives them the courage to stand against even the most disturbing and powerful foes without succumbing to the fear that would seize the hearts of those who lack such resolve. These fanatical followers also fight with forthright ferocity, rushing headlong into combat and striking without regard for their own well-being. Their only purpose is the greater cause they serve, even if that cause demands their death.

#### Militant Tactics
Militants use their Frenzied Longsword under the following circumstances:
* If the opponent would already have advantage on attacks against the Militant.
* To draw an opponent’s focus onto the Militant. This is especially effective when another Militant is near death. The opponent must then choose whether to finish off the wounded Militant (and take a large amount of damage) or leave the wounded Militant alive, allowing it to keep fighting while the opponent is forced to deal with the vulnerable Militant.

### Wolf Warriors
Rare are the Wolf Warriors, devoted to the White Wolf, god of winter and war. These regiments drape themselves in pelts of alabaster fur over battered and worn armor. They grip blades engraved with savage beasts upon brass hilts, and emblems of snarling wolves mark their shields. When loosed upon the fields of war, these men emulate the hunting techniques of primal canids, working in savage unity to bring down their foes.
Most infamous is the moment these regiments join together and unleash a chorus of hunting war-cries akin to the howls of wild lupines. 

\columnbreak

<br>

Delivered with such devotion and doggedness, the cries often cause foes to turn tail and retreat before ever locking weapons with the daunting pack of soldiers.


#### Wolf Warrior Tactics
Wolf Warriors use the same tactics as Militants, except they use their Frenzied Longsword in combination with Battle Howl. The disadvantage from being Frightened cancels out the advantage granted by their attack. It is also important for them to keep other allies alive so that their Pack Tactics feature remains active.

> ### Traits
>
> ***Fanatical Devotion.*** The swordsman has advantage on saving throws against being Charmed or Frightened.
>
> ***Pack Tactics.*** The swordsman has advantage on an attack roll against a creature if at least one of its allies is within 5 feet of the creature and the ally is not Incapacitated.
>
> ### Actions
>
> ***Longsword.*** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 17 (3d8 + 3) Slashing damage.
>
> ***Frenzied Longsword.*** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 25 (5d8 + 3) Slashing damage. Hit or miss, attack rolls against the swordsman have advantage until the start of its next turn.
>
> ***Battle Howl (Recharges after a Short or Long Rest).*** The swordsman lets out a terrifying howl. Each other Wolf Warrior Swordsman in a 90-foot emanation that is not Incapacitated may expend a use of this ability to bolster the howl. Each hostile creature in the emanation must then make a *Wisdom Saving Throw:* DC 10 + 2 (Max of 20) for each other swordsman that contributed to the howl. <br>
> *Failure:* The target is Frightened of the swordsman and each other swordsman that contributed to the howl for the next minute. It can repeat the saving throw at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on a success. <br>
> *Failure by 5+:* While Frightened, the creature can't cast spells other than cantrips, can't concentrate, and can make only one attack if it takes the Attack action.

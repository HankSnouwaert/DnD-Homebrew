# Knight Compendium 1.0

Among the champions of the mortal world, few figures capture the imagination quite like the knight. Clad in gleaming armor, mounted on a powerful steed, and bound by sacred oaths to causes greater than themselves, knights stand as humanity's answer to the monsters, tyrants, and dark powers that threaten to unmake civilization. They are the sword arm of kingdoms and gods alike, and the mere sight of a knight riding forth under a noble banner has been enough to scatter warbands and restore hope to villages that had long since abandoned it.

Yet knighthood is not merely a matter of armor and weaponry. It is a way of life, forged through years of grueling training, rigid discipline, and the slow accumulation of hard-won experience. A fresh-faced errant and a grizzled veteran of a hundred campaigns may both bear the title of knight, but the gulf between them is vast enough to swallow entire armies. The youngest knights ride into battle burning with ambition and the desire to prove themselves, their courage not yet tempered by the weight of genuine loss. The oldest have seen enough of war and sacrifice to understand exactly what their oaths demand of them, and they ride forth anyway.

What separates the knight from the common soldier is not equipment, though their arms and armor are typically of the finest quality, nor is it birth, though many knights come from noble stock. It is their code. 

\columnbreak


Every knight, regardless of the order they serve or the god they revere, is bound by a set of principles that governs not only how they fight but how they live. Honor, duty, and courage are the pillars upon which knighthood is built, and a knight who abandons these principles does not merely disgrace themselves; they become something altogether more dangerous: a warrior of exceptional skill and training utterly unmoored from conscience.

Not all knights serve the forces of light and order. For every champion who dedicates their life to the protection of the innocent, there exists a dark reflection — a fallen knight who has turned their formidable gifts toward cruelty and domination, or a black-armored warrior who serves powers of shadow and corruption with the same zealous devotion that their counterparts offer to the divine. These dark knights are among the most feared figures in the world, for they combine the discipline, martial excellence, and unwavering resolve of true knighthood with purposes that inspire only dread.

At their greatest, however, knights transcend the merely martial and become something approaching myth. The most exalted among them, those who have endured trials that would shatter lesser souls and emerged bearing the blessings of angels or ancient powers, become living legends whose deeds reshape kingdoms and whose names are spoken with reverence for generations. Such figures are more than warriors. They are symbols, proof that mortal courage and virtue, carried far enough, can stand against even the darkest forces the world has to offer.

## Knightly Rankings
The following are representations of knights across a range of rankings and power levels.

### Knight Errant
Young nobles who wish to walk the path of knighthood and become figures of legend often begin as Knight Errants. These eager youths have typically spent their childhood mastering all manner of weapons and memorizing countless codes of conduct. Some may be taken into service by a lord or seasoned knight to begin their journey as Errants.

Despite their rigorous study, such recruits are inexperienced and lack the refined discipline forged through years of hardship and conflict that veteran knights possess. Many are brazen and overconfident in their martial and diplomatic abilities, often disregarding the advice and warnings of their elders. They expect reverence and respect from all they encounter.

For all their pompous behavior, even novices to knighthood remain formidable adversaries. These hot-blooded warriors fight with ardent ferocity, eager to prove their worth and earn a place among the heroes of old. Knights in higher standing often admire such youthful enthusiasm, seeing it as a necessary stage on the path to full knighthood, one that is inevitably tempered by the hardships of sacred duty.

### Knights of the Realm

An Errant who has proven themselves through years of faithful service or through a great feat of heroism may claim the title of Knight of the Realm, becoming a fully recognized knight in the eyes of their superiors. Such exalted individuals have not only honed their combat skills to great proficiency but have also refined their conduct, allowing them to maneuver skillfully through the political landscapes of noble courts while presenting an air of majesty to all who behold them.

Many Knights of the Realm belong to great orders pledged to the service of a god or kingdom, acting as elite forces when dire threats arise. When the security of the state or the sanctity of divine law is challenged, these knights ride forth gallantly to put an end to such villainy.

Others are granted stewardship over small estates, ruling from modest castles or manors and charged with protecting nearby villages and farmers from roaming bandits and beasts. In return, the common folk pay taxes and offer loyalty to their lord. Most knights are capable of crushing entire bands of robbers single-handedly, and even savage beasts such as ogres or minotaurs have fallen before these valiant defenders, a lance piercing through blubbery fat and corded muscle alike.

### Questing Knights
Many knights grow restless in their roles as ministers of the state or high-ranking soldiers and yearn for a greater purpose. Whether driven by the desire to have their names sung by bards for generations or compelled by visions and whispers from transcendent powers, these knights abandon their worldly possessions save for armor, weapons, and mount, and set forth upon a sacred quest.

Some wander aimlessly across the realms, seeking signs to guide them toward their higher calling. These brave nomads often become local heroes, slaying entire warbands of orcs or beastmen single-handedly, driving their swords through nightmarish foes and casting them back into the hells from which they came. Others delve into ancient tombs and forsaken ruins to retrieve holy relics from ages long past.

On occasion, groups of Questing Knights travel together in pursuit of a shared goal. Such bands are capable of extraordinary feats, liberating villages from the tyranny of dark lords and their minions, or standing against dozens of well-armed raiders who ravage the countryside.

### Blessed Knights
When a Questing Knight proves their valor through trials and hardships that would break lesser warriors, they may draw the attention of higher beings. Angels, great fey, or other divine entities may visit such champions and grant them mystic boons in recognition of their selfless deeds.

These blessings can grant strength surpassing that of a troll, longevity far beyond that of mortal men, and a will resilient enough to withstand even the darkest temptations. Blessed Knights become figures of renown; wherever they appear, crowds of commoners gather to offer praise in hopes of receiving a blessing themselves. Royal courts grant them full attention, hanging on every word they speak.

Many Blessed Knights become stewards of hallowed lands such as enchanted meadows, sacred groves, or forgotten chapels, purging any impurity that dares to set even a taint claw upon their domain. Others devote themselves to protecting the weak, seeking out the greatest evils that plague the land, and eradicating them utterly.



> ##### Knight Champion

## Knightly Actions

If you wish to make your knight a true solo threat, you may grant it an extra Knightly Action it can use each round of combat, increasing its CR by 1.

This grants the knight an additional action in combat, taken on Initiative Count 21 (losing ties), separate from the knight’s normal initiative. On that count, the knight can take one of the following actions, provided it is not Incapacitated; it may still use Tenacious Will even while Incapacitated.

You may choose to grant the knight additional Knightly Actions on Initiative Count 15 and Initiative Count 0 (losing ties). For each additional action granted this way, the knight’s challenge rating increases by 1.

**Charge.**  The knight, or controlled mount it is riding, moves up to 20 feet without provoking opportunity attacks. The knight can make a melee attack with its lance at any point during this movement with the following properties: *Melee Weapon Attack:* Lance attack bonus to hit, reach 10 ft., one target. *Hit:* 10 (3d6) Piercing damage.

**Tenacious Will.**  If the knight is subject to an ongoing effect that allows a saving throw at the start or end of its turn (or as an action), it can immediately make that saving throw with advantage. On a success, the effect ends.

## Heroic Steeds
A great number of heroes have found their way into the annals of history through valiant deeds and the overcoming of impossible odds. Yet many of these warriors would never have succeeded in such daunting trials were it not for their equally remarkable steeds. Through countless harrowing ordeals faced together by mount and rider, only the most resilient and steadfast pairs endured and triumphed. Such mounts can overwhelm orc warriors with brute force or charge headlong at an adult dragon without a moment’s hesitation. This document contains a variety of heroic steeds knights may utilize, from elite horses to supernatural creatures such as pegasi and griffons.

### Noble Warhorses
Most knights ride out on trusty warhorses, relying on their strength and speed to carry them in and out of danger. Especially esteemed or privileged knights may ride upon noble warhorses whose power and stamina dwarf that of a typical steed. These horses belong to long lines of horse nobility, bred for their bravery and combat prowess. Knights riding upon these heroic steeds find they have a loyal and capable partner who will carry them through thick and thin.

### Imperial Pegasi
Imperial pegasi are the paragons of their kind and exceedingly rare. These awe-inspiring variations of the already elusive pegasus are creatures of grace, power, and nobility. Only the most valiant individuals are capable of earning the trust of these angelic beasts and soaring through the skies upon their backs.

The bond formed between rider and Imperial Pegasus is unbreakable once forged. A pegasus will carry out every command of its master without question and will even sacrifice itself to rescue its rider should they fall into grave peril.

#### Noble War Horse & Imperial Pegasus Tactics
When ridden by a creature wielding a lance (a very common pairing), these mounts favor one of two battlefield tactics:

**Hit-and-Run Harassment.** The rider and mount make a swift pass-by attack, striking from reach and then continuing their movement without provoking opportunity attacks. They retreat far enough that most enemies, especially melee-focused ones, struggle to retaliate.

**Shock Charge.**  The mount charges into melee and attacks with its hooves. The rider then withdraws slightly and follows up with a lance attack made with advantage. This tactic is particularly effective against ranged enemies, who are less able to respond with punishing opportunity attacks.

### Imperial Hippogriffs
Imperial hippogriffs are larger and far more aggressive than their more common kin. Some believe their immense size and ferocity stem from imperial griffon blood introduced at some point in their ancestry. Whatever the truth, these beasts are fiercely territorial and jealously guard their hunting grounds from any who would trespass upon them.

When such a regal monster lays claim to wide swaths of farmland, the consequences can be disastrous for local communities, as even a well-formed militia has little chance of driving it away. Many nobles, however, view the hippogriff as the perfect mount, a potent symbol of lordship. Any king or duke astride such a beast is a formidable presence upon the battlefield.

Acquiring such a prized mount is a trial unto itself, for hippogriffs must be reared from a very young age. As a result, ambitious royalty will pay a fortune for eggs or chicks. For every noble success story, many greedy peasants seeking quick wealth have been torn to ribbons attempting to sneak past the keen senses of protective mothers. Likewise, countless knights have tried, and failed, to slay a guarding matron, only to have a harpoon-like beak punch through steel armor to pierce flesh and bone.

#### Imperial Hippogriff Tactics

Hippogriffs are aggressive predators that focus on targets that provoke them. They close quickly, attempting to Grapple a creature with their Wicked Claws, then carry the victim aloft. While airborne, they strike with Puncturing Beak attacks before dropping the target once sufficient height is reached.

### Demigriffons
Within the forests and canyons of the world dwell some of the most majestic predators known, creatures sought by the greatest of knights and paladins. These beasts possess spirits of iron and deal swift, merciless death.

At first glance, the demigriffon may seem lesser than the true griffon, lacking the gift of wings. Yet any who witness a demigriffon’s fury soon learn otherwise, as a flurry of claws and beaks can cleave through entire regiments of goblins like a sickle through wheat. Shields and armor offer little protection; beak and talon punch through such defenses, pinning prey to the ground before tearing it apart.

Demigriffons live alone or in mated pairs for brief periods, hunting horses, aurochs, and other large game. Any predator that attempts to compete with a demigriffon will find a foe that fights to the death to drive away encroaching rivals.

To tame a demigriffon, a warrior must defeat the creature in combat, beating it into submission without killing it. Many reckless or extraordinarily brave fighters have attempted this feat. Even if a would-be rider should prevail, the beast may still reject them if it senses a lack of the same savage resolve burning within its own heart. Those who prove worthy gain a bold ally that charges through the thickest fray, carrying its rider out the other side.

#### Demigriffon Tactics
Demigryphs excel in sustained combat. They rush targets with low Dexterity, attempting to pin them in place. Once a foe is restrained, the demigryph exploits the disadvantage imposed by that condition to make its Puncturing Beak attacks far more lethal.

### Imperial Griffons
Few creatures symbolize chivalry and heroism more fully than the griffon. These magnificent beasts appear upon heraldry throughout mortal empires, their likenesses borne on banners fluttering in the wind as kingdoms clash and warriors raise shields emblazoned with the creature’s image.

It is no wonder that kings across the world have sought griffons as proof of their right to rule and of the might they command. As dynasties have risen and fallen, each monarch has pursued ever more marvelous beasts to cement their supremacy over rival crowns. Only the most robust and disciplined specimens are chosen to carry lord or lady aloft.

Over generations, these carefully bred griffons have grown larger and more courageous than their wild ancestors. Such pedigreed beasts are known as Imperial Griffons. When one descends amid the chaos of battle, both foe and ally are struck by its commanding presence. Such fearsome creatures can carve bloody paths through hardened troops, crushing all opposition beneath massive talons.

Should this display fail to make an enemy to plead and surrender, an Imperial Griffon may unleash a roar of overwhelming authority, one capable of driving common soldiers to their knees, forcing them to prostrate themselves before mount and rider.

Over time, distinct variations of Imperial Griffons have emerged as kingdoms sought to develop unique bloodlines to outmatch rival nations. These lineages are regarded as nearly as sacred as noble houses themselves. Decisions to crossbreed Imperial Griffons carry enormous political weight; such arrangements are often codified in treaties, much like dynastic marriages.

\columnbreak

The hatred between griffons belonging to rival nations can become so deeply ingrained over generations of warfare that, should noble griffons from opposing houses meet upon the battlefield, they will rush at one another without hesitation, tearing and rending until one is made to bow before the other.

#### Moon-Gaze Griffons
These nocturnal griffons bear the features of owls and are able to fly through the air without a sound. Their keen senses allow them to see through darkness and even illusionary magics, as nothing escapes their searching, steel-hard gaze. In battle, the beast weaves in and out of the fray, melding into the shadows before reappearing with talons outstretched to strike once more. The elven rulers of the deepest forests are said to ride such griffons, as their agility and silence allow their mounts to remain hidden.

#### Peregrine Griffons
The most agile of all griffons, the Peregrine Griffon bears the visage of an intimidating falcon. The combination of its powerful wings and sharply curved beak allows the creature to dive upon its enemies in a blur of speed, crashing into them with such force that even a full-grown war elephant would stagger from the blow. The most ruthless of warrior-kings and khans have favored these griffons as mounts, carrying them across vast distances during arduous military campaigns.

#### Resplendent Griffons
Bearing brilliant plumage and the squawking maw of a parrot, no other griffon can rival the ear-splitting cries of the Resplendent Griffon. Its deafening shrieks alone are said to be capable of killing a man. These clamorous creatures can also mimic any sound they have heard and, stranger still, often use this talent to mock and ridicule others. Such griffons are kept as prized pets by distant island kingdoms, and some say that the greatest pirate kings and queens ride above the waves atop these magnificent beasts.

#### Two-Headed Griffons
Rare are the griffons that bear two heads, each seeming to possess a mind of its own. No discord exists between the pair; instead, perfect cooperation, working together with the efficiency of an imperial regiment. In flawless synchrony, they tear through multiple foes in seconds, both heads constantly scanning the battlefield, ready to react to threats from any direction. As such, they have become the ultimate symbol of unity, and only the greatest empires can claim such strange and formidable creatures.

#### Griffon Tactics
Griffons are aggressive predators that close in on foes quickly, attempting to Grapple a creature with their Wicked Claws, then carry the victim aloft. While airborne, they strike with Puncturing Beak attacks before dropping the target once sufficient height is reached.

If surrounded or facing clustered enemies, a griffon will first attempt to use Bloody Roar to scatter groups threatening it or its allies. If it cannot affect three or more targets with this ability, it reverts to the aerial grapple tactic above.

**Moon-Gaze Griffons.** A Moon-Gaze Griffon that fails to grapple a target withdraws into the darkness, repositioning and waiting until the following turn to strike again.

**Peregrine Griffon Tactics.** A Peregrine Griffon darts in and out of combat at extreme speed. Once it successfully Stuns a target, it immediately follows up by Grappling that creature.

**Resplendent Griffon Tactics.** If a Resplendent Griffon cannot reach a target or wishes to suppress a ranged attacker while carrying a Grappled creature, it uses its mocking call ability to harass and disrupt enemies from the air.

**Two-Headed Griffon Tactics.** A Two-Headed Griffon is far less selective with its Bloody Roar, unleashing it even if only two enemies can be affected.

### Imperial Griffon Variants

The Imperial Griffon can take any of the following upgrades. Each upgrade increases the griffon’s CR by 1 unless otherwise stated.



#### Moon-Gaze Griffon

- The griffon doesn’t provoke opportunity attacks when it flies out of an enemy’s reach.
- The griffon gains Darkvision and Truesight out to 120 feet.
- The griffon can take the Hide or Search action as a Bonus Action, and it gains a +5 bonus to the ability check made as part of that action.

#### Peregrine Griffon

- The griffon’s Fly Speed increases by 20 feet.
- If the griffon moves at least 40 feet straight toward a target immediately before hitting it with a Rend attack, the attack deals an extra 13 (4d8) Piercing damage instead of Slashing damage. The target must succeed on a *Constitution Saving Throw:* DC 19.  
   *Failure:* The target is *Dazed** until the end of its next turn. While Dazed, the target can’t concentrate and can cast only spells of 1st level or lower.  
   *Failure by 5+:* While Dazed, the target is also *Stunned*.

**New condition described on previous page*

\columnbreak

#### Resplendent Griffon

- The griffon can mimic any sounds it has heard, including voices. A creature that hears the sounds can determine they are imitations with a successful DC 19 Wisdom (Insight) check.
- The griffon’s Blood Roar affects a 60-foot cube instead of a 30-foot cube. In addition, creatures within 15 feet of the griffon make the saving throw with disadvantage and take 13 (3d8) Thunder damage on a success or a failure.
- The griffon can replace one of its Rend attacks with a mocking call that forces a creature of its choice within 90 feet that can hear it to make a *Wisdom Saving Throw:* DC 19.  
  *Failure:* The target takes 18 (4d8) Psychic damage and has disadvantage on the next attack roll it makes before the end of its next turn.


#### Two-Headed Griffon

- The griffon makes one additional attack when it uses its Multiattack.
- The griffon can’t be Blinded, Charmed, Frightened, Dazed*, Stunned, or Surprised.
- The griffon’s Blood Roar recharges after 2 rounds instead of using its normal recharge.
- The griffon can take two reactions. It can’t use more than one reaction on the same turn, and it regains both reactions at the start of its turn.
- The griffon's CR is increased by 2 rather than 1

## New Holy Aura Spells
The following are new holy aura spells for paladins. Some of them serve as alternatives to existing paladin spells.

#### Aura of the Crusader
*3rd-level evocation*
___
- **Casting Time:** 1 Bonus Action
- **Range:**  Self
- **Components:** V
- **Duration:** Concentration, up to 1 minute
- **Classes:**  Paladin

You emit a radiant aura of divine power in a 30-foot radius centered on yourself. While within the aura, you and your allies deal an extra 1d4 Radiant damage when you hit with a weapon attack or unarmed strike.

***Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot.*** When you cast this spell using a spell slot of 4th level, the extra damage increases to 1d6. When you use a spell slot of 5th level or higher, the extra damage increases to 1d8.

#### Aura of Hope
*3rd-level Evocation*
___
- **Casting Time:** 1 Bonus Action
- **Range:**  Self
- **Components:** V
- **Duration:** Concentration, up to 1 minute
- **Classes:**  Cleric, Paladin

An aura radiates from you in a 30-foot Emanation for the duration, you and your allies have Advantage on Wisdom saving throws and Death Saving Throws and regains the maximum number of Hit Points possible from any healing.

#### Aura of Might 
*3rd-level evocation*
___
- **Casting Time:** 1 Bonus Action
- **Range:**  Self
- **Components:** V
- **Duration:** Concentration, up to 1 minute
- **Classes:**  Paladin

You radiate an aura of might in a 30-foot radius centered on yourself. While within the aura, you and your allies have advantage on Strength ability checks and Strength saving throws.

Additionally, when you or an ally within the aura shoves a creature, creatures being pushed can be pushed up to 20 ft. away instead of 5 ft., and creatures being knocked prone can't stand until the end of their next turn unless they use all of their movement to do so.

#### Aura of Shielding
*4th-level evocation*
___
- **Casting Time:** 1 Bonus Action
- **Range:**  Self
- **Components:** V
- **Duration:** Concentration, up to 1 minute
- **Classes:**  Paladin

You emit a defensive aura in a 30-foot Emanation. While in the aura, you and your allies have Half Cover.

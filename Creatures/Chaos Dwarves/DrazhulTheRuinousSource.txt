## Drazhul the Ruinous
*A Daemonsmith Wielding a Fiendish Soul-Trapping Crucible*<br>
**Habitat:** Desert, Mountain, Underdark, Urban, Volcanic; **Treasure:** Arcane <br>

In the treacherous politics of the demon smiths, only the most shrewd and ruthless can secure a position among the ruling clergy. All fel dwarfs hunger for power, each driven to attain, or outright seize, positions of authority. Yet the higher one climbs, the more perilous the ascent becomes, for the upper echelons are dominated by the most ancient dwarven practitioners, beings who have ruled for countless years.

Even among these megalomaniacal spellcasters, most bow before such malignant dictators, content to cling to whatever domains they have claimed and avoid the wrath of their tyrannical superiors. But Drazhul's craving for political dominance and sorcerous mastery could not be quelled by threats or the promise of horrific punishment.

Drazhul's rise was swift, faster than that of any other young demon smith. He had already begun forging pacts and alliances with influential powers within the empire. The elders were not blind to the impact this young artificer had made in so short a time, and they quickly realized the danger he posed to their dominance if left unchecked.

Thus, the schemes of the most powerful demon smiths came crashing down upon him, tearing apart all that Drazhul had built and casting him out as a pariah. He barely managed to avoid outright assassination at the hands of his rivals. Instead, he was exiled to the Great Black Spire, the furthest outpost of the fel dwarf Ashen Territories-a place constantly assailed by monstrosities from the Corrupted Wastes, as well as savage armies of orcs, ogres, and chaos beasts seeking to break through and pillage the factory cities of the fel dwarfs.

Most demon smiths cast into such a remote corner fall prey to the ceaseless violence that besets the region. 

\columnbreak

<br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br>

Yet despite the danger and instability, Drazhul did not merely survive; he thrived. He defied the fate of those before him, rising to become master of the Spire itself and commander of its armies.

Even in this distant fortress, far removed from the vast resources of the empire, Drazhul's command of the dark arts has only grown. His influence has expanded as he has sought out knowledge unknown even to the most ancient demon smiths. He has collected and forged a plethora of grim artifacts, including the Hellshard Amulet, his spite made manifest, which bathes his vengeful strikes in infernal fire, and the Graven Sceptre, a massive iron mace engraved with the names of every previous master of the Black Spire, capable of felling even the mightiest beasts with a single blow.

Most powerful and nightmarish of all his relics, however, is the Daemonspite Crucible. Forged from cursed gold and infused with demonic corruption, Drazhul has become permanently bound to this relic. It ferociously tears the souls from those around him, dragging them into the Crucible to be ravaged by the Infernal Tormenters, terrifying specters that inhabit the cursed object. With claws of smoke and anguish, these fiends liquefy the essence of their victims, refining it into pure arcane power to fuel Drazhul's wicked spells.

Now, word has reached him that the elders' grip on power is waning. Drazhul seeks to enact his vengeance and claim his place as the undisputed lord of all fel dwarfs. Astride his great Baleful Taurus, Ashbellow, and armed with tools of occult devastation, few could hope to stand against such a tyrant.

Yet it is rumored that the Crucible to which he is bound is intertwined with his very essence. Should this heirloom be destroyed, it would bring about his ultimate end.

### Drazhul's Lair

Drazhul plots within the Great Black Keep, a fortress standing at the farthest reaches of the Fel Dwarf Empire. It was built to withstand the relentless assaults of those who seek vengeance against the kingdom's cruelty. Behind its battered iron walls, molten rock courses through the fortress like blood through veins, while the stench of sulfur and stale smoke hangs heavy through its halls.

The fel dwarves who dwell within are among the most formidable of their kind. Drazhul's relentless pursuit of wicked artifice has tainted both the land and the air. Even speaking his name is said to invite misfortune. The natural world writhes under his influence, and the very elements are driven into rancor, lashing out at any who dare venture through the abused landscape.


#### Regional Effects

The area where Drazhul dwells is corrupted by his presence, creating one or more of the following effects everywhere within 5 miles of his lair:

- Elementals that spend more than 24 hours within the area must make a *Wisdom Saving Throw:* DC 19 or become Charmed by Drazhul for the next 24 hours or until they leave the area. While Charmed, they are Lawful Evil, become violent, and attack any non-Elementals and non-Fel Dwarfs on sight.
- Whenever a creature says Drazhul's name in the area, it takes 1d4 Fire damage as its mouth fills with hot ash. Drazhul may choose for a creature to not be affected this way.
- Whenever a creature makes a blacksmithing check in the area, it can roll a d6 and add the number rolled to the check. Additionally, if the check was made to repair or build a weapon, the creature may sacrifice 500 gp worth of metal. If it does, the weapon gains one of the following effects at random for the next 24 hours or until the creature imbues another weapon with an effect this way:

<br>

> ##### New Condition: Obsessed 
> The creature becomes fixated on a creature or object, which becomes a source of its obsession. Being obsessed has the following effects on a creature:
> * While the creature can see a source of its obsession, it can't target any creature or object with an attack, spell, or ability except for a source of its obsession.
> * While the creature can see a source of its obsession, it can't cast spells or use abilities that target an area (such as the *fireball* spell) unless a source of its obsession is in that area. 
> *  The creature can't willingly move away from a source of its obsession.

**New condition described below*



> ##### New Condition: Fatigue
> The creature experiences debilitating weariness that can accumulate in levels. Each time a creature gains the Fatigue condition, its Fatigue Level increases by 1. If the level reaches 6, the creature dies. Fatigue Levels have the following effects on a creature:
> * **d20 Rolls:** When a creature makes a d20 Test, subtract its Fatigue Level from the d20 roll.
> * **Spell Save DCs:** Subtract a creature's Fatigue Level from the Spell Save DC of any spell it casts.
> * **Speed** A creature's speed is reduced by a number of feet equal to 5 x its Fatigue level. This can't lower its speed below 5 feet.
> 
> **Ending the Condition:** Completing a Long Rest reduces a creature's Fatigue Level by 1.
> 
> **Fatigue vs. Exhaustion:** Fatigue is an alternative mechanic to the Exhaustion condition, and any ability that removes levels of Exhaustion or grants immunity to it does the same for Fatigue.

### Daemonspite Crucible

The Daemonspite Crucible is an artifact bound to Drazhul, granting him terrible power. It is a Tiny sentient Chaotic Evil magic artifact with an Intelligence of 20, a Wisdom of 10, and a Charisma of 18. It is attuned to Drazhul and cannot be attuned to any other creature. It has hearing and Blindsight out to a range of 120 feet. He always has the artifact with him, and it has the following traits.

***Demiplane.*** The Crucible contains a demiplane that is an empty room 30 feet in each dimension. Within the demiplane, three Infernal Tormenters are bound to the plane under the Crucible's control. The Crucible is aware of anything that happens in the demiplane. If Drazhul dies or becomes Petrified, the Soul Eater effect ends and any souls within the demiplane are returned to their bodies.

***Soul Eater.***  Drazhul the Ruinous may choose to activate the Crucible when he rolls Initiative or at the start of his turn. It then remains active for the next minute or until he dies or becomes Petrified.

While active, on Initiative Count 30 (losing ties), the Crucible attempts to draw out the soul of any number of creatures within 90 feet of it. Any creature may choose to let its soul be drawn out. If more than one creature accepts, the Crucible chooses one. 

If none accept, each creature other than Drazhul and Ashbellow within a 90-foot emanation originating from the Crucible must make a *Wisdom Saving Throw:* DC 22. The Crucible may then choose one creature that failed the saving throw and tear its spirit from its body, placing it in the center of the demiplane. If there is already a soul in the demiplane, the effect fails. The body left behind is Unconscious and in a state of suspended animation. 

The spirit resembles the creature's mortal form in almost every way, replicating its game statistics and possessions; possessions created this way lose any magical properties and disappear if they leave the creature's person. Any damage or other effects that apply to the spirit also affect the physical body, and vice versa. When the effect ends, the spirit returns to the body.

At the end of its turn, the affected creature can repeat the saving throw. On a success, the spirit returns to its body and the effect ends. The spirit also returns to the body if Drazhul the Ruinous dies or becomes Petrified, or if *Dispel Good and Evil* is cast on the target.

***Destroying the Crucible.*** The Crucible is destroyed if all three Infernal Tormenters are dead at the same time.

### Drazhul and Ashbellow
You can pair Drazhul the Ruinous with his steed, Ashbellow, to turn him into a true endgame threat. Together, they have a combined Challenge Rating (CR) of 24.

> ##### New Condition: Dazed 
> A dazed creature can’t take reactions and on its turn, it must choose whether it gets a move, an action, or a bonus action; it gets only one of the three.

\columnbreak

<br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br>

> ##### New Condition: Resolute
> The creature stubbornly digs in to weather any assault. It experiences the following effects:
>  * Attack rolls made against the creature have disadvantage.
>  * The creature can't take its move action on its turn.
> * The creature makes Strength checks and all saving throws with advantage, except Dexterity saving throws, which it makes with disadvantage.
> 
> The condition ends if an effect moves the creature against its will, knocks it prone, or the creature becomes charmed, frightened, or incapacitated. A creature that is prone, charmed, frightened, or incapacitated can't become Resolute.

<br>

> ##### New Condition: Burning
> The creature has been set ablaze by something that continues to damage it each round until it is extinguished. The default source described here is fire, but other sources could deal different types of damage, such as acid. 
> * **Default Damage:** A burning creature takes 1d4 fire damage at the end of each of its turns unless the source that inflicted the condition states otherwise.
> * **Ending the Condition:** As an action, the burning creature can extinguish the fire by dropping prone and rolling on the ground. The fire also goes out if it is doused, submerged, or suffocated. For example, another creature could use an action to put out the flames with their cloak or by pouring water on them.
> * **Multiple Sources of Burning:** If a burning creature would gain this condition again from another source, the new burning effect only replaces the old if it deals more damage each round. Otherwise, it is ignored.

> ## Infernal Tormenter

### Drazhul – Combat Tactics
#### General Strategy
If no creature willingly offers its soul to Drazhul's Crucible, Drazhul selects a creature vulnerable to melee attacks, allowing the Infernal Tormenters to overwhelm it.

Drazhul prefers to remain in a defensible position where he can stay Resolute and accumulate Power for spellcasting. He avoids entering melee whenever possible, resorting to his Graven Sceptre only when he lacks sufficient Power to cast spells.

If Cruel Backlash affects him, Drazhul withdraws from combat if possible and waits for the effect to expire before re-engaging.

#### Spell Priorities
##### Divine Hammer
* Cast if it can hit three or more targets.

##### Wall of Fire
* Cast if it can affect two or more targets.
* Prioritize opportunities where the spell can also heal Ashbellow.

##### Magmatic Stone
* Use against creatures concentrating on spells.
* Also use if it can hit three or more targets.
* Can be used to heal Ashbellow when appropriate.

##### Earthen Rupture
* Cast if it can hit three or more targets.

##### Hellish Rebuke
* Cast when damaged by a creature with low Dexterity.

##### Scorching Ray
* Only cast when Drazhul has no Power available and cannot reach a target with his Graven Sceptre.

\columnbreak

### Ashbellow – Combat Tactics
When accompanied by Ashbellow, Drazhul becomes more aggressive, advancing into the heart of battle while remaining Resolute. Together they prioritize:

**Fire Absorption** – Ashbellow benefits from fire-based effects, including Magmatic Stone Storms and Fire Wall, which can restore his health.

**Low-Strength Enemies** – They focus on targets least capable of resisting their attacks.

**Large Enemy Formations** – They seek opportunities to inflict maximum damage across clustered foes.

#### Ashbellow's Abilities
Ashbellow selects its abilities according to battlefield conditions:

##### Searing Gust
* Preferred when existing Magmatic Stone Storms can be exploited.
* Ashbellow will avoid consuming all active storms, as they can continue to provide healing.

##### Molten Shockwave
* Preferred when existing Earthen Rupture pillars can be exploited for greater effect.

## New Spells

#### Divine Hammer
*6th-level evocation*
___
- **Casting Time:** 1 action
- **Range:** 150 ft
- **Components:** V, S
- **Duration:** Instantaneous
- **Classes:**  Cleric

Choose a point you can see on the ground within range and a direction. A giant hammer manifests and slams the ground at the chosen point, impacting everything in a 10-foot cube centered on the point. It then sends a 30-foot-long, 10-foot-wide shockwave in the chosen direction before disappearing.

Each creature in the area of the hammer’s impact or shockwave must make a Dexterity saving throw. On a failed save, a creature takes 5d6 fire or lighting damage (your choice) and 5d6 thunder damage and is knocked prone; it is also dazed* until the end of its next turn if it is in the area of the impact. On a successful save, a creature takes half as much damage and isn’t knocked prone or dazed.

#### Earthen Rupture
*4th-level Evocation (Druid)*
___
- **Casting Time:** Action
- **Range:** Self (60-foot Line)
- **Components:** V, S
- **Duration:** Instantaneous
- **Classes:**  Druid

You rend asunder the earth in a 5-foot-wide line up to 60 feet long. Creatures touching the ground in the area must make a Strength saving throw. On a failed save, they take 4d6 Bludgeoning damage and fall Prone. On a successful save, they take half as much damage and don't fall Prone. Large or larger objects and structures in the area automatically fail the save and take twice as much damage. 

The ground in the area becomes Difficult Terrain until cleared. Each 5-foot-square portion requires at least 1 minute to clear by hand.

**Earthen Pillar.** A pillar of stone bursts from the ground centered on the far end of the line. The pillar has a diameter of 5 feet and a height of up to 40 feet. The ground where the pillar appears must be wide enough for its diameter, otherwise, it doesn't appear. The pillar has AC equal to your spell save DC and 25 hit points. When reduced to 0 hit points, the pillar crumbles into rubble, creating an area of Difficult Terrain with a 10-foot radius that lasts until cleared.

**Creatures Caught On The Pillar.** If the pillar is created beneath a creature, that creature must succeed on a Dexterity saving throw or be lifted by the pillar. A creature can choose to fail this save.

If the pillar is prevented from reaching its full height by a ceiling or other obstacle, a creature on the pillar takes 3d6 Bludgeoning damage and is Restrained, pinched between the pillar and the obstacle. The Restrained creature can use its action to make a Strength or Dexterity check (its choice) against your spell save DC. On a success, the creature is no longer Restrained and must either move off the pillar or fall from it.

***Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot.*** The damage of both effects increases by 1d6 for each slot level above 4th.

#### Magmatic Stone
*3rd-level Evocation (Sorcerer, Wizard)*
___
- **Casting Time:** Action
- **Range:** 100 feet
- **Components:** V, S, M (a bit of igneous rock)
- **Duration:** 1 round

You conjure a molten rock and hurl it at a creature within range. Make a ranged spell attack against the target. On a hit, the target takes 4d6 Bludgeoning damage. Whether the attack hits or misses, the rock explodes on impact, creating a 15-foot-radius sphere of whirling magma centered on the point of impact. Its area is Lightly Obscured and Difficult Terrain. The sphere spreads around corners and seeps through openings at least 5 feet in diameter.

At the start of your next turn, the sphere moves 30 feet in the direction you hurled it and dissipates. Any creature in the sphere when it first appears, and any creature whose space the sphere enters or passes through, must make a Dexterity saving throw. On a failed save, the creature takes 4d6 Fire damage. On a successful save, the creature takes half as much damage.

***Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot.*** The Bludgeoning and Fire damage both increase by 1d6 for each slot level above 3rd.

#### Subjugation
*2nd-level enchantment*
___
- **Casting Time:** 1 action
- **Range:** 60 ft
- **Components:** V
- **Duration:** Concentration, up to 1 minute
- **Classes:**  Bard, Sorcerer, Warlock, Wizard

You attempt to crush the will of one creature you can see within range. Constructs and undead are immune to this effect. The target must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw or become frightened of you until the spell ends. While frightened in this way, the target is also charmed by you.

The frightened target can repeat the saving throw at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on itself on a success.

***At Higher Levels.*** When you cast this spell using a spell slot of 3rd level or higher, you can target one additional creature for each slot level above 2nd. The creatures must be within 30 feet of each other when you target them.

## Barrow Knights
*Ancient Knights Risen Alongside Their Undying Steeds*<br>
**Habitat:** Underdark; **Treasure:** Armaments <br>
In ages past, only the greatest of tribal warriors rode upon the backs of horses. Such beasts were powerful symbols of wealth and status, and only chieftains and their most celebrated champions could claim them. Clad in crude bronze armor and wielding lances suffused with mystic enchantments, these horsemen served as devastating shock troops, capable of crashing through the ranks of unmounted soldiers with terrifying force.

Upon a warrior’s death, their mount was sacrificed, and the two were buried together. Rider and steed were sealed beneath great earthen mounds, fully clad in their battle attire, so that they might continue their conquests in the afterlife.

Over long centuries, the tombs of these ancient warriors may become inundated with dark magic, seeping through the soil and into the bones of rider and steed alike. If enough of this tainted power accumulates, the metal-clad remains shudder back to life. The awakened corpse mounts the remnants of its once-proud beast and strides forth from its grave, wreaking havoc upon the world once more as a Barrow Knight.

These unholy raiders exist only to slaughter any living thing they are able to run down. They fight with the same finesse and ferocity they displayed in life, their martial skill undiminished by death. The enchantments upon their weapons are twisted and amplified by the necromantic magic that drives them forward. 

\columnbreak

<br><br><br>

A wound inflicted by a corroded lance carries a dreadful curse, preventing natural recovery unless proper remedies are applied.

Barrow Knights often arise in small war bands. Many ancient crypts that house these monsters are long forgotten, only for villages to be built unknowingly nearby, until the dead rulers of ages past awaken and reclaim the land. At other times, more devious minds orchestrate their return. Necromancers, liches, and vampires actively seek out barrows, using dread incantations to bind these fallen warriors to their service. Such undead riders make excellent cavalry for the armies of the damned.

### Barrow Reavers
The most dangerous of all Barrow Knights are known as Barrow Reavers. In life, these animated remnants were the most ruthless of marauders, preying upon the weak without guilt or restraint, pillaging and slaughtering with remorse. Even in death, their bloodthirsty avarice lingers. Their hexed lances are able to rob a creature of its vitality, siphoning life force to grant the foul bandit a sudden surge of vigor. So profound is the corruption of their souls that Barrow Reavers are frightfully resilient. When the rider’s or its mount’s bones are broken and sent flying by a well-placed blow, the damage is only temporary. Dislodged fragments shudder and crawl back into place, or are dragged through the air by unseen forces, knitting themselves together and renewing the undead menace once more.

### Barrow Knight Tactics
Barrow Knights and Reavers favor hit-and-run tactics, charging in and out of combat as they move across the battlefield. 

\columnbreak

<br>

They seek to curse as many enemies as possible before focusing their attacks on the most heavily injured targets. If a Barrow Knight is reduced to 0 hit points, it will redirect any remaining attacks to itself to preserve its mount.

### Barrow Kings

The greatest chieftains and kings of old commanded whole legions of mounted warriors to glory, as these proud rulers trampled their foes beneath a tide of thundering hooves and bronze-shod lances. Such was the authority of these monarchs that even centuries after they were entombed within their cairns, those who gaze upon the remains of the fallen king, still clad in full panoply, are overcome by his lingering primacy.

When these conquerors are drawn back into the world of the living by wicked magics, their terrible eminence draws other undead to follow the reanimated liege. Warbands of barrow knights ride wordlessly at the Barrow King’s side; no sound is spoken between master and servant as they go forth to bring death to the kingdoms that rose in their former lord’s absence.

\columnbreak

<br><br><br>

When such ebon-armored horsemen lead the charge of undead cavalry, it is a dreadful sight to behold. Howling gale-souls swirl around the riders, and the skull of the king glows pale and grinning, loosing a soul-rending howl. Such an onslaught fills any warrior facing the charge with a terror strong enough to kill the weak outright, or cause even the most stoic of soldiers to lose their nerve and flee.

#### Barrow King Tactics
Barrow Kings open battle with their Onslaught of Shadows, using the ability to reposition allied forces and disrupt enemy formations before committing fully to the fight.

### Barrow Griffons

Rare was it in the days of old for a king to soar into the heavens upon the back of a griffon, for only the most formidable rulers could tame such wild and proud monsters. From atop these winged terrors, rival tribesmen were plucked from their mundane steeds and torn asunder, and whole war bands were driven back by the pairing of mythic beast and extraordinary warrior.

When such champions were laid to rest, whether by a well-placed blow upon the field of battle or after a long life of  conquest,their loyal companions would perch atop their stone caskets, as phlegmatic as statues as the tomb was sealed by the king’s loyal subjects, along with the wealth that beast and rider had accumulated over their years together.

Those who later dared to excavate these resting grounds would find the remains of the griffons still seated in vigil, their bodies reduced to bone draped with tattered skin. Should such intruders attempt to pilfer the belongings of the deceased pair, remains of the guardian creak as it rises once more into unlife, roused by the need to defend its partner. 

\columnbreak

<br><br>

It lunges forward, beak and talons ready to slice and gouge, skeletal wings splaying wide as they beat the stale air, releasing a noisome gust that steals the strength from any who draw breath.

Worse still, the loyalty that revives the griffon from its eternal slumber may also rouse its rider from the grave. Together, these champions of old set forth to destroy those who defile their sanctum. Once provoked, the undead may not return to their rest, and instead may venture forth into the world, seeking glory once more as they did in the days of old.

#### Barrow Griffon Tactics
Barrow Griffon opens with their Decrepifying Gale to weaken enemies and then hit them to Grapple a creature with their Wicked Claws, then carry the victim aloft. While airborne, they strike with Puncturing Beak attacks before dropping the target once sufficient height is reached.

> ##### New Condition: Dazed 
> A dazed creature can’t take reactions and on its turn, it must choose whether it gets a move, an action, or a bonus action; it gets only one of the three.

> ##### New Condition: Quickened
> A Quickened creature gains an additional action on each of its turns. That action can be used only to take the Attack (one weapon attack or unarmed strike only), Dash, Disengage, Hide, or Use an Object action. The *haste* spell applies the Quickened condition among its other effects, and a creature cannot benefit from the Quickened condition from multiple sources at once.

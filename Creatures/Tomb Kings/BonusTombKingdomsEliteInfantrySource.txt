## Scion of the Dunes

Twisting through the dry landscape, brass blades clutched in bleached hands slice through the air, effortlessly passing through flesh and leaving trails of crimson steam in their wake as blood is boiled by the murderous heat these weapons emanate. Those caught in the torrent of death attempt to fight against this storm of destruction; arrows fly, swords sweep, and axes strike, but such acts are repelled by stinging winds and tides of lashing sand that rise to protect the Scion of the Dunes, a never-ending source of such mayhem and death.

\columnbreak

<br><br>

These skeletal figures were once rangers attuned to the harsh wastes. Such nomads were fiercely sought by pharaohs to lead their armies through the torrid wilderness and to fight on their behalf, for few could match the savage grace such champions brought to the battlefield. Many, for their service, were given opulent funerals surpassed only by those of royalty. Now the remains of these lethal servants have awakened, greeted by the blistering heat of their homeland, unforgiving forces ready to lend their aid once more to the chosen of the desert.

### Sion of the Dunes Tactics

Sions of the Dunes are masters of controlling the battlefield. They open with a wall of sand, cutting off opponents. They can cast *Wall of Sand* and then use two attacks to fling their enemy’s weapons through the wall or use *Wind Blast* to push large groups of targets through the wall.

## Tomb Herald
Anyone who would seek to end the immortal reign of the mummified sovereigns of the Tomb Kingdoms has an arduous task ahead. For even if they are able to sweep through the sea of skeletal soldiers, thwart the shrewd tomb priests’ necromantic incantations, and topple the ensouled stone titans under the undead lords’ sway, one must still face the final line of defense of such tyrants: the Tomb Herald.

These walking cadavers were once the chosen champions of the pharaoh in life, hand-picked from the ranks of his most elite warriors. To be chosen for such a task was the greatest honor one within the ranks of the pharaoh’s servants could achieve, as only one could ever carry this title at any given time.

\columnbreak

<br><br>

Such a chosen vassal’s life and soul were bound to their master’s. The herald would heedlessly intercept any manner of lethal blows meant to fell his ruler and do battle with any foes set against him. So militant was this devotion that when a king finally did die, a herald would allow themselves to be sacrificed and embalmed. Gleaming brass armor was laid onto their treated corpse and enchanted blades placed in their cold grip, so that they could be entombed next to their liege’s own mummified remains and continue to fight for their honor in the world beyond.


When the lords of old returned to the living to command their armies once more, the remains of their most trusted retainers rose from their graves to continue their oaths.

## Soul Shepherds

To beckon a single spirit from the afterlife into the land of the living is no minor task, and raising an entire congregation that moves freely among the living is a mighty feat indeed. Yet such feats of necromancy are routinely performed within the halls of the Tomb Kingdoms by withered figures who bear both flail and crook; their mere passage causing the veil between realms to thin.

These mystics are the Soul Shepherds, specialized members of the lich-priest hierarchy charged with gathering and commanding hordes of specters for rituals that demand vast reserves of power. In battle, they are equally formidable, capable of unleashing ethereal waves of death upon advancing foes, vaporous talons that seize and tear apart anything in their path. 

\columnbreak

<br><br>

Their implements can also inflict dreadful curses: binding victims in transparent chains that drain all hope of escape, or flooding the mind with pernicious whispers that cloud judgment and leave the afflicted wandering in a haze.



### Soul Shepherd tactics

Soul Shepherd sends their Flock of Souls at the largest group of enemies, then uses their two attacks on two different creatures: Crook of Binding to keep ranged attackers from escaping the path of their flock, and their Fail of Control to have low-wisdom melee fighters walk right into their storm of souls. They use Soul Tether if they are unable to reach any targets with melee attacks.

#### Guiding Wind 
*1st-level enchantment*
___
- **Casting Time:** 1 action
- **Range:** 60 feet
- **Components:** V, S, M (A legume seed)
- **Duration:** Instantaneous
- **Classes:**  Druid, Cleric
___

You summon a potent gust of wind, forming a 10-foot-radius sphere centered on a point within range. This wind then moves in a straight line up to 30 feet in a direction you choose. When the sphere initially forms, any Medium or smaller creature or unsecured object within its area is lifted and carried by the wind for the distance it travels. Creatures can choose to resist and remain stationary instead of being carried by the wind.

***At Higher Levels.*** When you cast this spell using a spell slot of 2nd level or higher, the sphere can travel an additional 10 feet for each slot level above 1st.

#### Wind Blast
*3rd-level evocation*
___
- **Casting Time:** 1 action
- **Range:** 90 feet
- **Components:** V, S, M (A legume seed)
- **Duration:** Instantaneous
- **Classes:**  Druid, Sorcerer, Wizard
___

You summon a powerful gust of wind emanating from a 20-foot-radius sphere centered on a point within range and blasting out in the direction of your choice. Medium or smaller objects in this area that aren't secured and aren't being worn or carried are pushed up to 15 feet in the chosen direction. Each creature in the area must make a Strength saving throw. On a failed save, a creature is pushed in the chosen direction and knocked prone. The wind also disperses gas and vapor and extinguishes candles, torches, and similar unprotected flames in its area.

***At Higher Levels.*** When you cast this spell using a spell slot of 4th level or higher, the wind pushes creatures and objects an additional 5 feet for each slot level above 3rd.

> ##### New Condition: Dazed 
> A dazed creature can’t take reactions and on its turn, it must choose whether it gets a move, an action, or a bonus action; it gets only one of the three.

<br>

> ##### New Condition: Fatigue
> The creature experiences debilitating weariness that can accumulate in levels. Each time a creature gains the Fatigue condition, its Fatigue Level increases by 1. If the level reaches 6, the creature dies. Fatigue Levels have the following effects on a creature:
> * **d20 Rolls:** When a creature makes a d20 Test, subtract its Fatigue Level from the d20 roll.
> * **Spell Save DCs:** Subtract a creature's Fatigue Level from the Spell Save DC of any spell it casts.
> * **Speed** A creature's speed is reduced by a number of feet equal to 5 x its Fatigue level. This can't lower its speed below 5 feet.
> 
> **Ending the Condition:** Completing a Long Rest reduces a creature's Fatigue Level by 1.
> 
> **Fatigue vs. Exhaustion:** Fatigue is an alternative mechanic to the Exhaustion condition, and any ability that removes levels of Exhaustion or grants immunity to it does the same for Fatigue.

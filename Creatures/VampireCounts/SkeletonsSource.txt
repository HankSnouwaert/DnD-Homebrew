## Skeletons
*Ossified Evil*<br>
**Habitat:**  Planar (Shadowfell), Underdark, Urban; <br> **Treasure:** Any <br>

Throughout the breadth of the world lie the discarded remains of many forgotten warriors where one would not imagine them. Beneath the verdant prairies and rolling hills, even in the dense forests of old oak, one can find the informal tombs of such soldiers, their bones and corroded armaments scattered and sunken deep into the earth after decades, or even a millennium.

When a corrupting presence plunges deep into the soil, these bygone corpses rise through the muck. Their separated bones are ensnared by the malevolent power and held together until the fully forged skeleton breaches the topsoil and rises to its feet, brandishing rusted buckler and blade.

Skeletons do not possess deeper reasoning or the ability to learn. The dark nature of their creation also imbues them with an impulse to attack the living on sight. Those controlled by practitioners of the forbidden arts act as obedient thralls, questioning no order they are given and following whatever command they are instructed to carry out to the best of their limited knowledge, until they are destroyed or given a new directive.

This complete compliance does not mean they possess no thought. Many talents of their former lives remain held deep within their remains and can be drawn upon when such skills would aid them in their given objectives. A skeleton that was once a thief in life, if told to reach the other side of a locked door, might attempt to pick the lock rather than break through the barrier.

Most importantly to the majority of handlers who create such vassals, skeletons are capable of wielding weapons effectively, unlike their fleshbound zombie brethren, who only shamble and flail. A skeleton set to cut down an opponent can adjust its strikes if it becomes obvious that its method of attack is ineffective, and it can also avoid immediate, evident danger, unlike a zombie that would remain oblivious to its own oncoming destruction.

Those skeletons that rise and wander without purpose may begin to perform instinctual actions from their former occupations. Some have been found sitting and sewing moldering linen, or mining forgotten caverns; others play cards in destroyed taverns, or dance in dusty ballrooms to melodies long faded.

### Flesh Carvers

Many necromancers prefer to craft their legions using the remains of those who possessed an understanding of martial prowess or spent their lives steeped in conflict. The unholy magics that animate the remains have a vile will of their own, seeking to inflict harm, and they pull such dangerous talents to the forefront so that the undead are capable of cutting down the living and spreading death across the land.

As such, when the bones of particularly cruel and merciless individuals are drafted through these dark rites, the bloodthirst cravings they held in life are stirred once more, along with the impulse to cut down any living creature in their path. Such anomalies are known as Flesh Carvers. They seem to possess a knack for delivering horrid injuries to those they fight. Some propose this stems from a form of joy in their past existence at inflicting pain and suffering that still lingers in the cadaver; an equally twisted explanation is that these acts are done out of some sort of envy for the flesh they once had.

Such prowess has made these creations highly sought after. Necromancers may seek out the burial sites of particularly brutal and warmongering tribes in hopes of securing the remains of such violent fighters. Other times, the threads of dark magic will stir what remains of long-dead invaders entombed in the soil, causing them to take up arms and march tirelessly, continuing the slaughter they once took part in so long ago.

> ##### New Condition: Wounds
> While most creatures are wounded in combat, in this context, the wound is a special form of injury that causes severe bleeding over a short period of time and only specialized attacks and effects can cause wounds. A creature can suffer from multiple wounds at once and at the end of its turn, a wounded creature takes 2 (1d4) necrotic damage for every wound it has sustained. Only creatures with blood (or something equivalent to blood) can suffer wounds. Wounds can be removed in the following ways:
> 
> * When a creature is healed, one wound is removed for every 5 hit points of healing it receives. 
> * A creature can expend one use of a healer's kit to remove two wounds from itself or another creature. 
> * A wounded creature, or another creature within 5 feet of it, can make a DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check as an action to remove one wound on a success. For every 5 points by which the check exceeds the DC, an additional wound is removed.

### Skeleton Servants
Some butlers, maids, and other retainers were so wholly devoted to their masters and committed to their work that it suffused their very being. It is no shock, then, that when the carcasses of such laborers are animated, the diligence they cultivated in service is retained.

Though their minds are not as nuanced as they were in life, a skeletal servant is able to execute and achieve far more complicated tasks than their common counterparts. They are also far more perceptive of changes in their environment, making them more dangerous to intruders. They can even coordinate with others of their kind, whether in battle to overwhelm an opponent, or to perform greater projects such as keeping a castle clean or a library organized.

Many dark wizards keep these caretakers as assistants in their sanctums to carry out a myriad of chores. Other times, such skeletons will return from the dead along with their patron, often when men or women of influence return as ghosts, revenants, or banshees. The graves where former staff were laid to rest will stir with movement as bones shuffle, ready to do their master’s bidding.

### Skeleton Mages
Just as a skeleton may still hold the ability to wield a blade, tie a sailor’s knot, or tend to a fire, there are those skeletons that once wielded magic before their vile rebirth and still carry that power into their unlife.
However, in the case of most of these skeletal mages, the full breadth of their arcane mastery obtained during their years of life has eroded away with their flesh, reduced to a single spell which they are able to cast. This is not to downplay their danger. Few sights are more daunting for a group of adventurers than a band of skeletons appearing from the gloom, skulls expressionless as their bleached hands conjure and hurl bolts of flame at the unlucky travelers. 

### Skeleton Tactics
Skeletons target first any creature that is most effective at dealing Bludgeoning damage.

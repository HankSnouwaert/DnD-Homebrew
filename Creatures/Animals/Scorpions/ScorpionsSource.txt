## Scorpions
*Venomous Death Dealers of The Desert*<br>
**Habitat:** Desert, Forest, Grasslands, Hills; <br> **Treasure:** None <br>
Few creatures inspire the same instinctive dread as the scorpion. Found lurking beneath rocks, within desert sands, and inside the boots of travelers who failed to check before dressing, these armored arachnids are among the most ancient and adaptable predators in the natural world. Though most species are small enough to fit in the palm of a hand, what they lack in size they more than compensate for with their venomous sting, which can bring down creatures many times their own weight.

***Ambush Predators.*** Scorpions are not pursuit hunters. They are patient, opportunistic, and disinclined to expend energy they don't need to. A scorpion will spend the better part of a day motionless beneath a rock or buried just below the surface of the sand, waiting for vibrations in the ground to signal the approach of something worth eating. When prey draws close enough, the scorpion moves with surprising speed, seizing it in its claws before driving its stinger home.

&emsp;***Venom and Armor.*** The scorpion's exoskeleton is a formidable piece of natural engineering, providing protection that belies the creature's small frame. Coupled with its venom, which varies dramatically in potency from species to species, the scorpion is equipped both to avoid becoming a meal and to secure one. Some scorpion venoms produce little more than localized pain, while others are capable of inducing paralysis, organ failure, or death in creatures far larger than the scorpion itself. Alchemists and poison-crafters prize scorpion venom highly, and the dangerous business of harvesting it from living specimens is a trade practiced in regions where scorpions are plentiful and coin is scarce.

***Desert Omens.*** In many cultures that make their homes in arid and desert environments, scorpions carry significant symbolic weight. They are seen variously as emblems of resilience, harbingers of death, or sacred creatures associated with gods of the underworld and poison. It is not uncommon for desert-dwelling peoples to incorporate scorpion imagery into their warriors' arms and armor, believing it to confer some measure of the creature's hardiness and lethality upon the wearer. Some nomadic tribes regard the unexpected appearance of a scorpion as a warning, and will break camp and move on rather than risk whatever ill fortune the creature's presence is thought to foretell.

***Deathstalker Scorpions.*** Despite its fearsome name, the deathstalker is a relatively slender and delicate-looking creature compared to many of its bulkier cousins. Its thin claws are weak enough that it cannot rely on them to restrain prey, which has driven the development of its primary asset: a venom of extraordinary and disproportionate potency. A deathstalker's sting has the potential to kill an adult humanoid, and smaller or more vulnerable creatures stand little chance of surviving an encounter with one unassisted. Paradoxically, the deathstalker's venom has also drawn the attention of healers and alchemists, who have found that certain compounds within it, carefully extracted and prepared, show remarkable medicinal properties. This combination of lethal danger and potential curative value makes the deathstalker one of the most sought-after scorpions in the world, and the specialists who make their living capturing them alive command steep prices for their services.

***Scorpion Swarms.*** A single scorpion is an <br> unpleasant encounter. A swarm of them is a <br> nightmare. When driven from their burrows by <br> flooding, magical disturbance, or simply the <br> malicious intent of someone who knows what <br> they are doing, scorpions will surge across the <br> ground in a living tide of claws and stingers. <br> Travelers caught in the open by such a <br> swarm face a relentless barrage of stings <br> from every angle, with no single point of <br> attack to defend against. Those who<br> survive the experience rarely forget the <br> sound: a dry, chittering rustle that <br> precedes the swarm like a whispered <br> warning. A deathstalker swarm is <br> considerably rarer and considerably <br> more lethal, and the mere rumor of <br> one in a region is typically enough <br> to empty a village overnight.

&emsp;***Giant Scorpions.*** In remote deserts, deep caverns, and the forgotten wilderness between civilized lands, scorpions can grow to monstrous proportions. A giant scorpion is a patient and methodical hunter, using its claws to seize prey and hold it fast while its stinger works with grim efficiency. Creatures caught in its grip have little recourse but to endure the venom and hope their constitution holds. Giant deathstalkers are rarer still, trading some of the bulk of their common cousins for greater speed and a venom so devastatingly potent that even large and hearty creatures can be reduced to a convulsing heap within moments of being stung. Warlords, dungeon builders, and those with a particularly creative approach to pest control have been known to make use of giant scorpions as guardians, though keeping them reliably contained is a challenge that has ended the careers of more than a few would-be monster tamers.

## Vultures
*Sinister Carrion Who Feed On The Dead & Dying*<br>
**Habitat:** Desert, Forest, Grassland, Hills, Mountain, Swamp; **Treasure:** None <br>
Vultures are large carrion birds that scour the land for carcasses to consume. Though there is great variety in the appearance of these scavengers, most have little to no feathers upon their heads and possess elongated necks. Such creatures are able to spot dead or dying animals from great altitudes, and they will swarm over decaying corpses, able to eat even the most putrid meat without becoming ill, in part due to their extremely acidic digestive systems.

### Giant Vultures
Giant vultures are birds larger than a full-grown man, with wingspans reaching up to twenty feet. Such creatures possess not only exceptional size but also extraordinary intelligence, nearly on par with that of humans. Possessing a shrewd cunning, a giant vulture will not attack creatures that appear strong and full of life, nor those surrounded by able-bodied allies.

Instead, they are opportunistic hunters, forming circling kettles high in the sky that strike only at wounded or exhausted prey to ensure its demise, tearing at their victim with spear-like claws and beaks, and, if need be, retching forth corrosive bile when their prey proves more difficult than first anticipated.

In desolate deserts and swamps corrupted by dark powers, it is not uncommon to see such flesh-eaters soaring through gloom-stricken skies, waiting patiently to descend upon those who have been sapped of their vigor by the hostile realm.

In other lands, however, these birds are sacred creatures believed to purify the land. Some clerics even keep them to hunt and dispose of undead and fiends, allowing the avian scavengers to feast upon unholy remains so that the fetid corpses will not taint the land any further.

#### Vulture Tactics
These scavenging birds rarely attack healthy prey. Instead, they circle high above the battlefield, waiting for creatures to become weakened or suffer from fatigue before striking.

Once a suitable target shows signs of fatigue or injury, the birds swoop down to harass it. They often begin an engagement by using their vomit attack, then quickly retreat out of reach. Afterward, they continue circling and making opportunistic attacks against the most fatigued creature.

## Dread Carrion
In the shadowed dunes and desolate plains of the eternal desert lie the remnants of the once-glorious Tomb Kingdoms. These vast lands are inhabited not only by the buried undead legions but also by the chilling specters of the skies: the dread carrion.

Dread carrion are monstrous, reanimated vultures bearing an unholy union of decayed flesh and preserved remains. The scale of these creatures is intimidating, with wingspans reaching that of several men laid end to end. Their desiccated skin clings tightly to their skeletal form, and their lifeless eyes gleam with an eerie light, signaling the dark magic that reanimates them.

In days of old, the giant carrion vultures were revered creatures, considered sacred to the denizens of the Tomb Kingdoms. Seen as messengers and intermediaries between the mortal realm and the heavens, they were embalmed upon their death and preserved with archaic rituals. Thus, the vast necropolises of the empire, home to its departed rulers and warriors, became the resting place for countless such vultures.

The Tomb Kingdoms may have been long silent, but they are far from lifeless. The powerful tomb priests, guardians of ancient rituals and forbidden magics, possess the capability to awaken the embalmed vultures. Summoned forth from their aeons of slumber, the dread Carrion soar the skies once more, providing invaluable aerial support to the ground-bound legions of the undead.

These creatures are not mere tools of war. They are deadly hunters of the arid desert, preying on wanderers and creatures already enfeebled by the scorching sun and relentless sandstorms. With dread inevitability, they descend upon the vulnerable, leaving little but bones in their wake.

But the dread carrion have evolved beyond mere talons and beaks as weapons. Through the dark rituals that bind them, they've gained the ability to spew forth a vile, necrotic filth. This foul substance weakens their prey, sapping them of strength and vitality, rendering them all the easier to subdue.

Among the dread carrion, there are those that stand a class apart. These are the blessed ones, chosen by the very gods of the Tomb Kingdoms. These sacred creatures are not only larger and more formidable, but they also possess the unique ability to release torrents of necrotic filth from high above. Like unholy bombers, they soar the heavens, raining down decay and death upon the enemies of the Tomb Kingdoms.

### Carrion of the Looming Sun
When the living inhabited what is now known as the Tomb Kingdoms, it was common for the nobility of those dynasties to face life-threatening journeys. Though the privileged possessed a higher standard of living than any others in the land, such rulers were still expected to traverse dangerous landscapes for diplomatic missions and royal affairs. Many princes and pharaohs perished in distant and unforgiving dunes, their remains seized by scavenging beasts.

Some dynasties offered sacrifices and prayers to a vulture-headed god said to decide the fate of those who crossed the harsh wilderness and to spare them from an early grave. Many pharaohs who practiced such rites would have giant vultures flying high above, following their caravans across the desolate seas of sand.

To an outside observer, these looming birds might seem like ill omens, silent stalkers waiting for a sovereign’s encroaching doom. Yet when calamity befell such a noble, the birds would swoop down to defend their charge. With sharpened beak and talon they would lash out at the pharaoh’s would-be murderer, or clutch the wounded ruler within their massive claws and carry them to safety.

When such a vulture died protecting its monarch, its body would be gathered by the royal priesthood and brought back to the king’s necropolis. There it would be embalmed with the most delicate care, and glyphs of warding, rivaling those placed upon the pharaoh himself, would be inscribed to preserve its remains. The mummified bird would then be placed within the future tomb of the pharaoh so that the loyal creature might continue to guard its master in the afterlife.

Now, as the mummified rulers rise from their sarcophagi, many are accompanied by the animated cadavers of their ancient saviors. These undead avians possess terrible power, their forms, preserved through sacred embalming, allow them to channel the wrath of the desert god against any who dare endanger their risen king.

Such protectors can unleash the radiance of the desert sun itself, calling down columns of oppressive heat and blinding light upon their enemies until they collapse beneath the sweltering glare. They are even able to brush aside deadly spells that would harm their allies and restore their fallen undead should they be destroyed.

### Dread Carrion Tactics
Dread carrion prefer to overwhelm prey that already appears weakened. They surround creatures with low Constitution or those suffering from fatigue, attempting to with their stench and attacks.
They usually begin combat with Spew Filth to debilitate their target. dread carrion use Feast, whenever the opportunity presents itself.

#### Sacred Carrion Tactics
Sacred carrion fight in much the same way as dread carrion, circling and harassing weakened enemies before committing to close combat.

The primary difference is their use of Filth Bombardment instead of Spew Filth. This ability allows them to weaken prey from a distance before closing in.

\columnbreak

#### Carrion of the Looming Sun Tactics
Carrion of the Looming Sun are most effective when accompanied by lesser undead allies.

At the start of combat they unleash Withering Pillar, seeking to inflict fatigue on multiple enemies. Once targets are weakened, they focus their attacks on creatures from that condition.

They remain close to their undead allies to benefit from Guardian’s Ward, and they use Stave Off the End to keep their companions fighting longer.

> ##### New Condition: Fatigue
> The creature experiences debilitating weariness that can accumulate in levels. Each time a creature gains the Fatigue condition, its Fatigue Level increases by 1. If the level reaches 6, the creature dies. Fatigue Levels have the following effects on a creature:
> * **d20 Rolls:** When a creature makes a d20 Test, subtract its Fatigue Level from the d20 roll.
> * **Spell Save DCs:** Subtract a creature's Fatigue Level from the Spell Save DC of any spell it casts.
> * **Speed** A creature's speed is reduced by a number of feet equal to 5 x its Fatigue level. This can't lower its speed below 5 feet.
>
> **Ending the Condition:** Completing a Long Rest reduces a creature's Fatigue Level by 1.
> 
> **Fatigue vs. Exhaustion:** Fatigue is an alternative mechanic to the Exhaustion condition, and any ability that removes levels of Exhaustion or grants immunity to it does the same for Fatigue. 

\columnbreak

> ##### New Condition: Burning
> The creature has been set ablaze by something that continues to damage it each round until it is extinguished. The default source described here is fire, but other sources could deal different types of damage, such as acid.
> * **Default Damage:** A burning creature takes 1d4 fire damage at the end of each of its turns unless the source that inflicted the condition states otherwise.
> * **Ending the Condition:** As an action, the burning creature can extinguish the fire by dropping prone and rolling on the ground. The fire also goes out if it is doused, submerged, or suffocated. For example, another creature could use an action to put out the flames with their cloak or by pouring water on them.
> * **Multiple Sources of Burning:** If a burning creature would gain this condition again from another source, the new burning effect only replaces the old if it deals more damage each round. Otherwise, it is ignored.

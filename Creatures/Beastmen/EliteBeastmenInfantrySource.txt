## Besthorn Impalers

Those who stand against the loathsome beastmen have found that these primitive creatures are effectively countered by artillery, long-ranged weaponry, and warriors atop winged mounts. Such tactics can decimate a herd of ferocious marauders, unless they have among their midst Besthorn Impalers.

Armed with sets of thick, cured javelins, these looming, horned figures can be seen in the back lines of wild raider armies. Each of their falcon-like eyes move independently, scanning the battlefield until their exemplary sight locks onto a distant archer or a winged steed soaring above. With a motion of their corded arms, they send javelins barreling forward with the force of a charging warhorse. When such piercing bolts collide with their intended target, the effects are devastating, capable of sending a pegasus or griffin spiraling out of control and crashing into the earth like a falling star.

Worse still is when they catch a glimpse of a fallen soldier, for within a heartbeat a javelin is sent plunging into the prone warrior, pinning them into the cold soil beneath. Held in place, the victim is left helpless as the Besthorn Impaler, or others of its kind, close in to claim their prey.

#### Besthorn Impaler Tactics
Beastmen Impalers prioritize flying targets. If no flying enemies are present, they focus on backline targets or enemies that are prone.

## Besthorn Moon Howlers
For the twisted mind of a beastman, even the most heinous and disturbing sights are rarely enough to scar its feral psyche or leave lasting trauma. Yet, on rare occasions, a beastman bears witness to something so incomprehensible that it shatters its cruel spirit, sending it into a delirium—babbling, losing all comprehension of the world around it, and gazing only upward at the gleaming moon to which it howls. Such lunatics are known as Moon Howlers.

What causes such a mental break in the autochthonous psyche of these creatures remains a mystery. Most scholars deem it safer to leave such questions unanswered for the sake of their own sanity. However their lunacy is triggered, wherever they go they spread madness among both men and beasts like fire through dry leaves.

Those afflicted are beset by sights and sounds mortal minds cannot comprehend. They hoot and laugh in fear and delight; limbs shake and twitch, and legs give way beneath them. Those who fail to resist these intrusive thoughts feel the same rage that burns within the beastmen consume them.

Moon Howlers are even deadly in combat, wielding crude weapons fashioned during their delirium—strange scythes cobbled together from rough-hewn branches and flint, imbued with incomprehensible incantations. Those struck by such weapons find insanity taking root within them, growing until it sears the mind.

### Besthorn Moon Howler Tactics
The Moon Howler uses Rush of Insanity on targets with the lowest Wisdom scores.

It may also use Rush of Insanity on allied beastmen that have more than half their hit points remaining and a CR of 1 or higher, granting them Primal Fury.

The Moon Howler prioritizes attacking enemies that are already Emboldened.

## Longhorn Prowlers
Longhorn Prowlers are among the most dangerous of beastmen, putting even their kin’s natural talent for ambush to shame. They move through darkness and foliage without so much as a leaf disturbed. Few are ever able to detect their presence before they unleash a flurry of hatchet blows.

These beastmen are utilized by Warhorns and Beast Lords as assassins, slipping behind enemy lines to lie in wait for the moment a general, wizard, or other important figure is most vulnerable. When such an opportunity presents itself, the Prowler erupts into sudden violence, axes held tight as it weaves between would-be guardians who attempt to impede its mission.

Within seconds of reaching its target, a blur of metal leaves the victim reduced to a heap of red ruin at its hooves before they are ever able to comprehend what has happened.

### Longhorn Prowler Tactics
Longhorn Prowlers are extremely fragile and prioritize stealth.

* If caught in the open by surprise, they immediately attempt to hide.
* They target isolated and fragile enemies, typically spellcasters such as wizards or bards.
* They remain hidden until striking, then use Action Surge to unleash a full set of attacks (up to four) with their axe against a single target.
* After attacking, they may use Bestial Cunning to retreat back into hiding.

## Besthorn Harbingers
When the dark gods set their schemes into motion and the children of chaos crawl forth from the deepest thickets, drawn by the will of malevolent deities, there come also the most deadly of all beastmen breeds: the Harbingers.

These grim figures are clad in tarnished black plate and wield great blades marked with runes and wrapped in all manner of fetishes. Hulking champions, they belong to no single clan or herd, appearing only at the command of their gods. They join other beastmen armies only in times of great cataclysm and turmoil, and only the most favored Beast Lords can command their zealotry, if only for a short time.

Few foes are more daunting to face. Their mastery of their ebon blades and their sheer ferocity allow them to best over half a dozen well-trained men at once. Their very presence permeates the land with primordial evil, as insects and vermin burst from the ground in grotesque homage to these servants of chaos, biting and burrowing into any who would defy them.

Should one survive the brutal blow of a Harbinger’s accursed blade, their mind is soon robbed of all humanity, leaving behind only the snarling instincts of a beast.

### Besthorn Harbinger Tactics
Besthorn Harbingers move into the densest group of enemies to affect as many targets as possible with their aura.

They then focus attacks on enemies with low Intelligence, using Devolving Smite. Spellcasters such as bards, warlocks, and clerics are preferred targets.

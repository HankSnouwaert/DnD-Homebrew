## Hydras
*Multiheaded Serpents of Legend*<br>
**Habitat:** Coastal, Forest, Mountain, Swamp; **Treasure:** Any <br>

Hydras embody unchecked growth and wild savagery in its most brutal form. A writhing mass of ravenous, beastly heads protrudes from a single colossal body, each snapping hungrily at anything within reach. Every crushing maw is powerful enough to snap a full-grown man in half with a single bite.

The hydra's most infamous trait is its rampant regeneration. Whenever one of its serpentine heads is severed, two more erupt from the bloodied stump, replacing the fallen head and ensuring the monster only grows more dangerous as the battle drags on.

Hydras become scourges upon whatever lands they inhabit, devouring any living thing unfortunate enough to wander within reach to sustain its assortment of heads. Yet despite their insatiable hunger, they are capable of long periods of dormancy. 

\columnbreak

<br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br>

They retreat into deep bogs, secluded <br> swamps, or hidden caverns, where they enter a <br> deep torpor for decades to conserve their strength while the surrounding land recovers and prey once again becomes plentiful.

The number of heads a hydra possesses fluctuates according to the resources available around it. Most spend the majority of their lives bearing five heads, but in times of famine they may tear off several of their own heads and consume the flesh themselves. Rather than regrowing the missing heads, the beast simply endures with fewer mouths to feed, shrinking to as few as a single head if starvation becomes severe.

Conversely, hydras that dwell in fertile lands or wage constant battle against powerful foes may increase far beyond their usual size, and such monsters become the subjects of local legend. The few survivors fortunate enough to escape their attacks tell of an endless sea of serpent heads <br> devouring everything in their path.

Many heroes seek out the domains of hydras, for permanently slaying one is considered a feat worthy of legend. Hydras are often drawn to prosperous farmlands, where abundant livestock and dense populations provide an endless source of food. After reducing entire settlements to lifeless ruins, the beasts leave behind untouched treasures, abandoned homes, and forgotten riches, awaiting any brave soul willing to challenge the monster that now claims them.

### Venomous Hydras

These hydra variants have heads resembling hooded snakes, and few sights are more intimidating than a quiver of their menacing maws surging forth, sinking their gleaming white fangs into their prey to inject a potent dose of venom. Even those who seek to avoid its deadly bite from afar with arrows or other ranged weapons soon learn that its heads are more than capable of spitting their lethal concoction, spraying viscous bolts to overwhelm their adversaries.

Though many of these beasts roam the wilderness of their own accord, most venomous hydras serve as the favored pets of wicked gods. They are often used as guardians of great artifacts or enchanted sites, lying dormant until awakened to bring a toxic end to any adventurers who seek the treasures entrusted to their care.

### Phytohydras

Phytohydras are strange, animated masses of tangled vines united into a single organism, with each sinuous stalk <br> ending in a large, cavernous maw. These writhing masses <br> of vegetation are active predators, seeking any source of nourishment to fuel their unending growth.

Mystery surrounds the origins of these bizarre plants. Legends claim they were created by a druidic conclave that sought to cultivate a creature capable of limitless growth. To achieve this, they watered seeds with vast quantities of hydra blood and mixed the ground bones of the many-headed beast into the soil. Soon, the strange nascent flora burst forth, proliferating beyond all control and overwhelming their creators.

Whatever their true origins, phytohydras pose a grave threat to any ecosystem or city in which they take root. A single phytohydra can overtake even the most well-defended settlements if it is not destroyed quickly and decisively. Should part of a phytohydra be severed from its main body and not burned promptly, the damaged plant will not only sprout a new head to replace the one that was lost, but the severed fragment will also take root, springing into an entirely new phytohydra within minutes. These newly <br> formed creatures immediately seek fresh prey to sustain <br> their relentless, unchecked expansion.

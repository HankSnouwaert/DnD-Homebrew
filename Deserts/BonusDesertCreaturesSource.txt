# New Desert Creatures


## Armadillofolk
*Hardened Nomads United Through Sacred Familial Bonds*<br>
**Habitat:** Desert, Forest, Grasslands, Hills, Swamps; **Treasure:** Relics <br>


Within tunnels carved into the hillsides of arid landscapes dwell a people who have endured the test of time again and again. The armadillofolk hold no value more sacred than the bond and defense of one’s siblings.

For these armored folk, this bond is inherent, as armadillofolk mothers always give birth to identical quadruplets of the same sex. Stranger still, these siblings share a connection known simply as “the Roll.” This bond psychically links the four together, allowing them to share thoughts and emotions. The psionic link strengthens as they age, enabling flawless coordination without a word spoken. With discipline, this power can even be used to subtly manipulate their own bodies.

\columnbreak

***Union of Bonds.*** Armadillofolk society is entirely structured around the value of one’s Roll. A set of quadruplets never willingly separates and makes all major decisions collectively.

Upon reaching adulthood, a Roll typically sets out into a nomadic life, relying solely on one another for survival. In time, female Rolls may settle if they discover a suitable place to carve a lasting settlement into the earth. Male Rolls continue wandering until they encounter a group of unattached female Rolls; at that point, both Rolls may consider union.

When determining such unions, individual attraction carries little weight. What matters is the compatibility of the two Rolls as unified entities. If the pairing is agreed upon, one male and one female from each Roll will mate, producing a new set of quadruplets.

***Shells and Loyalty of Stone.*** The armadillofolk worship no gods. It is said among them that they were created to mock and defy the gods themselves. The details of this ancient humiliation vary from Roll to Roll, each preserving its own version of which deities were mocked and how.

Having forsaken divine worship, their spiritual practices instead center upon the veneration of the Roll. Rituals are performed to strengthen familial bonds. Some take the form of prolonged meditations within special chambers, lasting for days, during which the minds of the four interlock so completely that the sense of individual self nearly dissolves. Others involve the creation of sacred artifacts: each member contributes to the crafting and psychically imprints upon the object as it is formed. Weaving agave fibers and shaping jade are among the primary crafts of these rites.

So sacred is the Roll that an armadillofolk individual will sacrifice anything to protect their brothers or sisters. They will endure, or even commit, grievous acts if it ensures the safety of their siblings.


***Battle Brothers and Sisters.*** Armadillofolk are generally peaceful and avoid confrontation, as well as prolonged relationships with other peoples, save for trading the gems they mine and the garments they weave.

However, should one member of a Roll be threatened, the aggressor must contend with the entire quartet until driven off or slain.

In battle, armadillofolk fight with deadly harmony. They execute precise maneuvers without speech, wielding jade weapons infused with psionic force and crafted by their own Roll. They can curl into armored spheres, deflecting lethal blows from their hardened shells. Using telekinetic force, they launch themselves forward, barreling into foes with devastating impact.

Even aerial opponents are not safe from these tactics. A Roll of armadillofolk can transform into a barrage of living missiles in an instant.

***Found Family.*** It is possible, through powerful ritual and the expenditure of great quantities of jade, for an armadillofolk individual to form a Roll with non-sibling creatures. Such an act is deeply controversial. Most armadillofolk consider bonding outside one’s birth Roll unnatural, even blasphemous. Those who attempt it usually do so out of desperation.

### Armadillofolk Tactics
Armadillofolk prioritize disrupting vulnerable backline targets, using javelins to pressure spellcasters and ranged enemies. If all four members of a Roll can coordinate, they will use Roll Out on a single target to quickly overwhelm it. This tactic is risky, as emerging from their shells costs a full action.

If a member becomes bloodied or overwhelmed, it will use Roll Out defensively to disengage and escape. Even while rolled up, armadillofolk remain useful by positioning adjacent to enemies to grant advantage to their allies’ attacks.

\pagebreakNum


### Armadillofolk Burrow Defender
Among the armadillofolk, it is crucial that each member of a Roll undertakes a distinct role to ensure the group’s continued survival. One may dedicate their life to gathering food, another to crafting tools and clothing. Often, one member devotes themselves to mastering martial weapons and military tactics, serving as the primary defender of the Roll.

When conflict arises, Rolls are often more organized and effective than formally trained military units.

\columnbreak

#### Armadillofolk Burrow Defender Tactics
Burrow defenders act as frontline anchors. They may roll into position alongside another defender to reinforce a defensive line.

If a burrow defender or nearby ally becomes bloodied, they will roll up and remain close, maintaining the benefits of Guardian Formation while reducing incoming damage.

### Armadillofolk Manipulator
Some armadillofolk are attuned to unseen forces that shape the world, able to sense the subtle gravitational pull of the earth itself. These individuals can harness this connection through the power of their Roll.

They are able to infuse stones with tremendous force. When thrown or launched, these stones can repel or draw in creatures near their point of impact. Most remarkable of all, they can teleport those with whom they share a mental link, provided all remain in contact with the earth.

\columnbreak

#### Armadillofolk Manipulator Tactics
Manipulators operate from the backline, controlling enemy positioning and battlefield flow. They use Gravity Slings to:

&#8226; Push enemies away to disrupt formations.

&#8226; Pull clustered enemies together for area attacks.

&#8226; Reposition allies, either pulling them into combat to overwhelm a target or pushing them out to safety.

They use Leyline Shift to escape melee or to swap positions with a frontline ally, reinforcing pressure where needed.

### Armadillofolk Thought Weaver
Among the most dangerous of their kind are the thought weavers, armadillofolk who have pushed the innate mental power of the Roll to its zenith.

They manipulate waves of force capable of tearing matter apart, crushing minds, and sending victims into comas, while simultaneously invigorating their siblings in battle. Their most insidious ability is the imposition of a twisted imitation of the Roll onto their enemies, binding the minds of their foes together so that any psychic harm inflicted upon one reverberates through them all.

\columnbreak

####  Armadillofolk Thought Weaver Tactics
Thought weavers begin combat by targeting multiple enemies, attempting to bind as many minds as possible to amplify group damage.
They prioritize:

&#8226; Mind Spike against targets with low Intelligence

&#8226; Psychic Headbutt against enemies concentrating on powerful spells (3rd level or higher)

They reserve *Here We Go* for moments when allies are fully positioned, maximizing the impact of a coordinated assault.

## Awakened Cacti
*Barbed Guardians of Precious Desert Water*<br>
**Habitat:** Desert; **Treasure:** None <br>
Even amid the dry sands and blistering heat of an inhospitable desert, life-giving magic can coalesce to suffuse the local vegetation with sapience, allowing them to move and speak with voices of their own. Most commonly granted such sentience are the cacti that litter these arid lands, uprooting themselves from the dry soil to stride across the parched wastes.

Such cacti are often awakened by druids to guard pockets of life scattered throughout the dunes, keeping watch for loathsome horrors that would despoil these precious sanctuaries. Such vast reserves can be as precious as silver or gold to desert tribes that struggle for survival, often warring bitterly over what little water can be salvaged.

### Cacti Warders
A Cacti warder may at first seem like an oddly shaped cactus, rising from the ground and unfurling its body into full view. It is a massive, barrel-chested creature supported by four thick legs, each ending in paw-like digits tipped with thorns. Its head bears jaws filled with rows of needle-like teeth, and the whole of its bulk is covered in waxy green skin layered with fine barbs.

Despite their bestial form, these creatures possess remarkable intelligence, acting as stewards and sages of oases hidden deep within the desert. Many adventurers seek out a cacti warder on spiritual pilgrimages, hoping for guidance. If the creature deems a traveler worthy, it releases psychedelic compounds that can liberate the mind, allowing one to delve deep into their own psyche.

Those who incur the ire of such a hazardous scholar, or attempt to take its vision-granting substances without permission, are swiftly punished. The warder can unleash a rain of piercing spines or capture victims with its needle-filled maw. The same hallucinogenic compounds it uses for guidance can also debilitate its foes; a breath of bizarre mist may send targets staggering in random directions or collapsing in bewilderment.

#### Cacti Warder Tactics
Cacti warders use Psychedelic Breath when they can catch three or more targets in the area. After using Psychedelic Breath, they follow up with Needle Burst under similar conditions, again aiming to strike at least three enemies.

Once their foes have been forced to spread out, Cacti Warders shift tactics and focus on isolating a single target—typically the one with the lowest Constitution. They concentrate their attacks on this creature to embed as many barbs as possible. If they successfully grapple the target, they drag it away from its allies, preventing others from coming to its aid.

## Giant Ant Lions
*Deadly Trap Masters of The Sands*<br>
**Habitat:** Desert, Grasslands, Hills, Underdark; **Treasure:** Any <br>

What might be mistaken for an otherworldly aberration hidden beneath the soil is, in fact, a natural beast enlarged to monstrous proportions. These creatures are known as giant ant lions, or sand trappers.

They are enormous insects with bloated abdomens and elongated heads ending in large, barbed pincers. Disturbing in appearance, these creatures grow to the size of an ox and use their multiple, branch-like limbs to instinctively construct masterful sand pit traps up to 20 feet deep and wide.

Though these pits may seem simplistic, they are ingeniously designed. The ant lion removes larger pieces of sediment, leaving only the finest grains of sand and dirt to line the rim of its trap, carefully shaping it to the angle of repose.

When prey steps onto the perimeter of the pit, the surface collapses under its weight, sending the unfortunate victim tumbling down the slope into the depths below. There, the lurking ant lion bursts through the soil, seizing its prey with its pincers and injecting an acidic venom that digests the victim from within.

\columnbreak

Small hairs covering the insect’s body anchor it within the surrounding sediment, allowing it to maintain its grip on struggling prey. If a particularly nimble creature manages to keep its footing amid the shifting sand, the ant lion will surface and use its head like a shovel, flinging dirt to destabilize the target and drag it into the pit.

Many tribes and civilizations living in or near badlands and deserts speak in hushed terror of the sand trappers, which feast on camels and unwary travelers crossing the arid landscape.

What most do not realize is that these voracious ambushers are merely the larval stage of another insect: the giant lacewing. These delicate, long-bodied adults are far less dangerous than their adolescent forms. After consuming enough prey, a giant ant lion undergoes metamorphosis into a lacewing, living only a few months. They migrate to greener lands, feeding on fruit and plant matter for the remainder of their short lives before mating. Before they die, they return to their arid homelands to lay their eggs beneath the soil, ensuring a new generation of hunters waits below.

### Giant Ant Lion Tactics
A giant ant lion waits patiently at the bottom of its pit for prey to approach the edge. If a creature does not fall prone and slide inward, the ant lion uses Wave of Debris to drag or knock the target toward the center.

It never leaves the safety of its pit, relying entirely on terrain control and forced movement to bring prey within reach.

## Warp Rippers & Arcana Wings
*Dimension Burrowing Horrors & Distorters of Magic*<br>
**Habitat:** Desert, Grasslands, Hills, Underdark; **Treasure:** Any <br>

Warp rippers are aberrations that twist the laws of nature to suit their own ends; an alien cunning lies beneath their insectoid guise, driving their voracious hunger for flesh.

These creatures can burrow through the fabric of reality itself, creating pocket dimensions in which they hide. Around these tears, gravity begins to shift, pulling inward toward the opening and snaring anything that comes too close. Victims are dragged toward the creature at the center of the disturbance, where the warp ripper can unleash gravitational shockwaves to crush those who resist its pull.

Once close enough, its mandibles pierce the target, injecting a strange substance that breaks down matter. The victim’s flesh twists and flickers in and out of reality until all that remains is a jittering mass of organic material, which the abomination consumes.

Such practitioners are nearly helpless against these creatures, as the very beating of the arcane wing’s wings dampens spells and can even halt casting entirely. When threatened, they can channel their stolen energy to teleport and unleash distorting waves of arcane force.

## Locusts
*Devouring Swarms Leaving Famine & Ruin In Their Wake*<br>
**Habitat:** Desert, Grasslands; **Treasure:** None <br>
Few mundane creatures rival the destructive force of a natural disaster, but locust swarms force to be reckoned with. When gathered in vast numbers, they scour the land as surely as any tornado or earthquake, leaving only barren earth in their wake. Fields are stripped bare, forests reduced to husks, and entire regions rendered lifeless.

Despite their apocalyptic impact, locusts arise through natural means. In times of abundant rainfall and rapid plant growth, certain grasshopper populations explode in number. Normally solitary, these insects are driven into constant contact by overcrowding. This triggers a rapid metamorphosis: their bodies enlarge, their coloration shifts to stark yellow and black, and their behavior becomes aggressive and insatiable.

The transformed swarm devours everything in its path—plants, crops, and even one another. As they migrate, their numbers swell into the billions, darkening the sky and consuming entire ecosystems. These migrations can persist for years, crossing continents and even oceans. In their wake lie famine, collapse, and the quiet ruins of once-thriving civilizations.

Ancient texts speak of such swarms as warnings and punishments sent to humble the proud and destroy the faithless. Entire empires are said to have fallen beneath their shadow.

When guided by a higher intelligence, the plague of locusts moves onto the largest group of spell casters to disrupt concentration and make as many attacks as possible. Beyond that, it acts without any regard for self-preservation.

## Divine Locusts
*Ruinous Heralds of Divine Punishment*<br>
**Habitat:** Desert, Grasslands; **Treasure:** Relics <br>
At times, the gods themselves weaponize the endless voracity of the locust.

When kingdoms fall into corruption or abandon their sacred rites, divine will may bend the natural order, calling forth locusts as instruments of judgment. Within these vast swarms emerge towering figures known as divine locusts, harbingers of famine and living avatars of divine wrath.

These beings stand over seven feet tall, shaped like humanoids but bearing the grotesque features of immense locusts. Their chitinous bodies are armored like plate, their compound eyes gleam with alien purpose, and their massive mandibles click with restless hunger. Long, segmented limbs end in blade-like tarsi, capable of cutting through armor and flesh alike.

Their wings beat with a deafening, chittering roar, filling the air with a maddening cadence that erodes the will of those who hear it. Few can stand firm as the swarm descends.

Divine Locusts are never solitary. They move in small cadres within the greater swarm, surrounded by countless lesser locusts. Though possessed of keen intelligence, they are utterly devoted to their divine purpose, showing no hesitation, mercy, or remorse.

A handful of these beings can consume entire harvests in hours. Armies that rise against them are cut down amid chaos and terror, their ranks broken by both blade and madness. Should one of these creatures fall, its kin descend upon it without hesitation, devouring the wounded angel alive to reclaim its strength and press the assault.

Even in death, they remain a threat. The corpse of a slain divine locust ruptures into a frenzy of smaller insects, lashing out at anything nearby before dispersing into the swarm.

Druids, in particular, regard divine locusts with deep hatred. Many sagas recount bitter wars between druidic circles and zealous clerical orders. When diplomacy fails, clerics may call down these celestial horrors to strip sacred groves and sanctuaries bare.

### Divine Locust Tactics
  
Divine locusts swarm vulnerable targets to gain advantage on their attacks. When one divine locust falls below 40 hit points, it moves into the largest group of foes to bring them in range of its death burst, and its allies will attack and consume it.

## Scorpionfolk
*Divine Guardians & Brutal Hunters of Ancient Antiquity*<br>
**Habitat:** Desert; **Treasure:** Armaments <br>
In the most distant sand-choked lands, where the mortal world brushes against stranger realms, dwell the formidable scorpionfolk. Those who glimpse them from afar, skittering across the empty wastes, often mistake the creatures for armored riders mounted upon strange beasts. Only when one draws closer does their true form become apparent. Their upper bodies resemble those of powerful humanoids clad in chitinous plates, while their lower halves are those of immense scorpions, their segmented tails arching high above them.

***Children of Discord.***
The scorpionfolk are among the oldest of races. They were wrought by a powerful god of chaos in an age before the shaping of the mortal world, when the gods themselves waged war for dominion over creation.

When their creator was finally cast down, the victorious powers of the cosmos claimed the remnants of that defeated army. Rather than destroy them, the gods bound the scorpionfolk to service, appointing them as eternal wardens of sacred places scattered across the planes.

Thus, the scorpionfolk became guardians of forgotten shrines, buried relics, and lands deemed too dangerous or too holy for lesser beings. Since that time, they have lingered in the desolate regions of the world, living sparse and primitive lives while keeping their ancient vigil. Rarely do they stray far from the hallowed grounds entrusted to their care.

***Hunters of Old.***
For thousands of years, the scorpionfolk have lived much the same way. Though they can live for nearly two centuries while active, they possess a strange survival adaptation. When food grows scarce, or their watch grows long, they burrow deep beneath the sands and enter a dormant diapause that can last more than twenty years. During this time, their metabolism slows to a near halt, and age touches them only faintly.

While dormant, they remain far from helpless. Scorpionfolk possess an innate tremorsense that allows them to perceive vibrations in the earth, a sense that becomes even sharper during their long slumber. The movement of prey or the approach of trespassers may awaken them from miles away.

When intruders wander too near their sanctified domain, the scorpionfolk rise from beneath the sands and strike with sudden violence, ambushing their quarry before it realizes it has been discovered.

***Bronze and Venom.***
Unlike mundane scorpions, the Scorpionfolk lack great claws. Instead, they wield curved bronze blades, crude weapons forged in simple bowl furnaces. With these hooked weapons, they catch and hold their victims, pinning them in place so their barbed tails can strike true.

The venom carried within their stingers is fearsome. Such a substance awakens a primal terror that seizes the mind. Even the hardiest warriors may find their limbs paralyzed within moments as their minds break under the venom’s assault, their bodies left helpless before the towering hunters.


\columnbreak

&emsp;Once prey has been subdued, the scorpionfolk consume as much as they can, gorging themselves upon the fallen. If any victims remain alive after the feast, they drag the comatose bodies back to their buried lairs beneath the sands. When such a bounty of food presents itself, the scorpionfolk may remain active for several years, mining stone, forging weapons, and raising their young, before once again returning to the silent depths below.

&emsp;***Imprints of the Gods.***
Much about scorpionfolk society remains poorly understood by other peoples. Though they are ruthless predators, their actions are guided by a strange and alien faith.

Scorpionfolk believe that the influence of the gods can be felt through the vibrations of the world itself. Just as their tremorsense allows them to perceive movement through the ground, they claim that places and objects touched by divine power emit subtle resonances in the earth.

Through this sense, they judge all creatures they encounter. Clerics and others clearly blessed by the gods may be spared, while those who appear to play no part in the designs of higher powers are often treated as nothing more than prey.

Yet this belief is not born of devotion or love. To the scorpionfolk, the will of the gods is simply another force of nature, no different from the shifting dunes or the movement of prey beneath the sand. It is merely a pattern in the world that can be sensed and followed.

***Scorplings.***
Scorpionfolk young, known as scorplings, are far more monstrous in appearance than their adult kin. Barely two feet long at birth, they are pale and soft-bodied, their eyes sealed shut as they rely entirely upon their heightened senses to navigate the world.

Each scorpling’s hands are encased in a large chitinous claw that is shed during its first molt. Though their stingers carry no venom, the young are swift and savage. They rush eagerly toward prey already paralyzed by their parents’ toxins, tearing into helpless victims with needlelike teeth and spike-tipped limbs.

Females produce litters of ten to fifteen offspring roughly a year after breeding, though they may delay birth by entering the species’ dormant state. Mothers carry their young upon their backs until their first molt, which occurs after roughly a year.

Some mothers remain within cool ruins or deep caves while others hunt, waiting for members of their brood to bring them food. Others roam the desert with their young clinging tightly to their armored backs, ready to spring forward the moment a creature collapses beneath their mother’s sting.

&emsp;***Scorpionfolk Champions.***
Among their kind, the largest and most formidable warriors are known as champions. These towering scorpionfolk wield enormous brass glaives whose sweeping blades can cleave a lion in two with a single stroke.

### Tug of War Contests
A tug-of-war contest is a special type of opposed Strength check between two creatures on either end of a rope.

While you're holding or tethered to a rope that is also held by or tethered to another creature no more than one size larger than you, you can use the Attack action to make an opposed Strength check against the other creature to try to pull the rope towards yourself. 

If you're able to make multiple attacks with the Attack action, this attack replaces one of them. 

If you are more than one size larger than the opposing creature, you automatically win this contest. If the results of both checks are higher than the rope's break DC, the rope snaps.

If you win the contest and the rope doesn't snap, you can pull the rope along with you as you move or pull it towards yourself while standing still. If the opposing creature doesn't drop the rope or is tethered to it, it will be pulled along as though you are grappling it. 

You can't engage in a tug-of-war contest using a hand that is holding other objects (including other ropes).

#### New Condition: Tethered
If a creature is attached to a rope (such as via a lasso, harpoon, or a secure tie), it is tethered to that rope. Creatures immune to the grappled condition can't be tethered. 

Any creature that succeeds in a tug-of-war contest against a tethered creature using the attached rope is considered to be grappling it. The tethered creature can end the grapple by untethering itself, breaking the rope, or winning another tug-of-war contest against the grappling creature.

### Scopionfolk Tactics
#### Scorplings and Scorpling Swarms
Scorplings are most effective when paired with their adult counterparts. They wait for a foe to become restrained or paralyzed before swarming in to attack. Adult scorpionfolk often carry scorplings into battle so they can release them the moment a vulnerable target presents itself.

#### Scorpionfolk 
Scorpionfolk prefer to lie in wait beneath the sand, ambushing their enemies from below. When combat begins, they rush targets with low Strength or Wisdom, attempting to subdue them with their khopeshes.

Once engaged, the scorpionfolk repeatedly attempt to sting their prey, hoping to frighten or incapacitate them. Each scorpion typically selects a different target. However, if a creature becomes paralyzed by a sting, the rest of the scorpionfolk immediately abandon their current quarry and converge on the helpless victim.

\columnbreak

#### Scorpionfolk Champions
Scorpionfolk champions thrive in the midst of large groups of enemies. They focus on paralyzing a target with their sting, allowing them to exploit their devastating critical hits against helpless prey.
If a creature is already paralyzed by another scorpionfolk, the champion immediately targets that creature, seeking to finish it off as quickly as possible.

#### Scorpionfolk Wrangler
Scorpionfolk wranglers specialize in dealing with foes that are difficult for other scorpionfolk to reach, such as flying creatures or highly mobile ranged attackers.

They begin by lassoing a target, pulling it closer while advancing. If the victim is still out of range of the wrangler's garrote, the wrangler continues the tug-of-war, foregoing its garrote attack, hauling the creature nearer until it can be seized and finished with a sting attack.

### Giant Vultures
Giant vultures are birds larger than a full-grown man, with wingspans reaching up to twenty feet. Such creatures possess not only exceptional size but also extraordinary intelligence, nearly on par with that of humans. Possessing a shrewd cunning, a giant vulture will not attack creatures that appear strong and full of life, nor those surrounded by able-bodied allies.

Instead, they are opportunistic hunters, forming circling kettles high in the sky that strike only at wounded or exhausted prey to ensure its demise, tearing at their victim with spear-like claws and beaks, and, if need be, retching forth corrosive bile when their prey proves more difficult than first anticipated.

In desolate deserts and swamps corrupted by dark powers, it is not uncommon to see such flesh-eaters soaring through gloom-stricken skies, waiting patiently to descend upon those who have been sapped of their vigor by the hostile realm.

In other lands, however, these birds are sacred creatures believed to purify the land. Some clerics even keep them to hunt and dispose of undead and fiends, allowing the avian scavengers to feast upon unholy remains so that the fetid corpses will not taint the land any further.

#### Vulture Tactics
These scavenging birds rarely attack healthy prey. Instead, they circle high above the battlefield, waiting for creatures to become weakened or suffer from fatigue before striking.

Once a suitable target shows signs of fatigue or injury, the birds swoop down to harass it. They often begin an engagement by using their vomit attack, then quickly retreat out of reach. Afterward, they continue circling and making opportunistic attacks against the most fatigued creature.

## Dread Carrion
In the shadowed dunes and desolate plains of the eternal desert lie the remnants of the once-glorious Tomb Kingdoms. These vast lands are inhabited not only by the buried undead legions but also by the chilling specters of the skies: the dread carrion.

Dread carrion are monstrous, reanimated vultures bearing an unholy union of decayed flesh and preserved remains. The scale of these creatures is intimidating, with wingspans reaching that of several men laid end to end. Their desiccated skin clings tightly to their skeletal form, and their lifeless eyes gleam with an eerie light, signaling the dark magic that reanimates them.

In days of old, the giant carrion vultures were revered creatures, considered sacred to the denizens of the Tomb Kingdoms. Seen as messengers and intermediaries between the mortal realm and the heavens, they were embalmed upon their death and preserved with archaic rituals. Thus, the vast necropolises of the empire, home to its departed rulers and warriors, became the resting place for countless such vultures.

The Tomb Kingdoms may have been long silent, but they are far from lifeless. The powerful tomb priests, guardians of ancient rituals and forbidden magics, possess the capability to awaken the embalmed vultures. Summoned forth from their aeons of slumber, the dread Carrion soar the skies once more, providing invaluable aerial support to the ground-bound legions of the undead.

These creatures are not mere tools of war. They are deadly hunters of the arid desert, preying on wanderers and creatures already enfeebled by the scorching sun and relentless sandstorms. With dread inevitability, they descend upon the vulnerable, leaving little but bones in their wake.

But the dread carrion have evolved beyond mere talons and beaks as weapons. Through the dark rituals that bind them, they've gained the ability to spew forth a vile, necrotic filth. This foul substance weakens their prey, sapping them of strength and vitality, rendering them all the easier to subdue.

Among the dread carrion, there are those that stand a class apart. These are the blessed ones, chosen by the very gods of the Tomb Kingdoms. These sacred creatures are not only larger and more formidable, but they also possess the unique ability to release torrents of necrotic filth from high above. Like unholy bombers, they soar the heavens, raining down decay and death upon the enemies of the Tomb Kingdoms.

### Carrion of the Looming Sun
When the living inhabited what is now known as the Tomb Kingdoms, it was common for the nobility of those dynasties to face life-threatening journeys. Though the privileged possessed a higher standard of living than any others in the land, such rulers were still expected to traverse dangerous landscapes for diplomatic missions and royal affairs. Many princes and pharaohs perished in distant and unforgiving dunes, their remains seized by scavenging beasts.

Some dynasties offered sacrifices and prayers to a vulture-headed god said to decide the fate of those who crossed the harsh wilderness and to spare them from an early grave. Many pharaohs who practiced such rites would have giant vultures flying high above, following their caravans across the desolate seas of sand.

To an outside observer, these looming birds might seem like ill omens, silent stalkers waiting for a sovereign’s encroaching doom. Yet when calamity befell such a noble, the birds would swoop down to defend their charge. With sharpened beak and talon they would lash out at the pharaoh’s would-be murderer, or clutch the wounded ruler within their massive claws and carry them to safety.

When such a vulture died protecting its monarch, its body would be gathered by the royal priesthood and brought back to the king’s necropolis. There it would be embalmed with the most delicate care, and glyphs of warding, rivaling those placed upon the pharaoh himself, would be inscribed to preserve its remains. The mummified bird would then be placed within the future tomb of the pharaoh so that the loyal creature might continue to guard its master in the afterlife.

Now, as the mummified rulers rise from their sarcophagi, many are accompanied by the animated cadavers of their ancient saviors. These undead avians possess terrible power, their forms, preserved through sacred embalming, allow them to channel the wrath of the desert god against any who dare endanger their risen king.

Such protectors can unleash the radiance of the desert sun itself, calling down columns of oppressive heat and blinding light upon their enemies until they collapse beneath the sweltering glare. They are even able to brush aside deadly spells that would harm their allies and restore their fallen undead should they be destroyed.

### Dread Carrion Tactics
Dread carrion prefer to overwhelm prey that already appears weakened. They surround creatures with low Constitution or those suffering from fatigue, attempting to with their stench and attacks.
They usually begin combat with Spew Filth to debilitate their target. dread carrion use Feast, whenever the opportunity presents itself.

#### Sacred Carrion Tactics
Sacred carrion fight in much the same way as dread carrion, circling and harassing weakened enemies before committing to close combat.

The primary difference is their use of Filth Bombardment instead of Spew Filth. This ability allows them to weaken prey from a distance before closing in.

\columnbreak

#### Carrion of the Looming Sun Tactics
Carrion of the Looming Sun are most effective when accompanied by lesser undead allies.

At the start of combat they unleash Withering Pillar, seeking to inflict fatigue on multiple enemies. Once targets are weakened, they focus their attacks on creatures from that condition.

They remain close to their undead allies to benefit from Guardian’s Ward, and they use Stave Off the End to keep their companions fighting longer.

> ##### New Condition: Fatigue
> The creature experiences debilitating weariness that can accumulate in levels. Each time a creature gains the Fatigue condition, its Fatigue Level increases by 1. If the level reaches 6, the creature dies. Fatigue Levels have the following effects on a creature:
> * **d20 Rolls:** When a creature makes a d20 Test, subtract its Fatigue Level from the d20 roll.
> * **Spell Save DCs:** Subtract a creature's Fatigue Level from the Spell Save DC of any spell it casts.
> * **Speed** A creature's speed is reduced by a number of feet equal to 5 x its Fatigue level. This can't lower its speed below 5 feet.
>
> **Ending the Condition:** Completing a Long Rest reduces a creature's Fatigue Level by 1.
> 
> **Fatigue vs. Exhaustion:** Fatigue is an alternative mechanic to the Exhaustion condition, and any ability that removes levels of Exhaustion or grants immunity to it does the same for Fatigue. 

\columnbreak

> ##### New Condition: Burning
> The creature has been set ablaze by something that continues to damage it each round until it is extinguished. The default source described here is fire, but other sources could deal different types of damage, such as acid.
> * **Default Damage:** A burning creature takes 1d4 fire damage at the end of each of its turns unless the source that inflicted the condition states otherwise.
> * **Ending the Condition:** As an action, the burning creature can extinguish the fire by dropping prone and rolling on the ground. The fire also goes out if it is doused, submerged, or suffocated. For example, another creature could use an action to put out the flames with their cloak or by pouring water on them.
> * **Multiple Sources of Burning:** If a burning creature would gain this condition again from another source, the new burning effect only replaces the old if it deals more damage each round. Otherwise, it is ignored.

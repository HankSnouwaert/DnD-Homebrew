# Desert Compendium
Deserts are any vast arid areas devoid of plentiful water. They are usually thought of as being hot, but can technically include frigid tundras. Indeed, many deserts experience wild temperature swings between day and night that are difficult to adapt to. Some deserts are made up of vast oceans of sand threatening to swallow any unprepared traveler, while others are rocky plains dotted with grand stone mesas and mighty canyons carved across their face. 

Regardless of their exact form, all deserts are extremely hostile to life and difficult to traverse. Their vastness, harsh weather, and lack of food or water mean travelers must take great care in their preparations, packing immense resources and extreme travel gear for the arduous journey. 

However, the brutality of the desert has also made it an ideal barrier for ancient fledgling civilizations wishing to avoid the predations of marauding hunter-gatherers. 

\columnbreak

<br>

Such civilizations grew around rivers and oases; they may or may not still exist, and the remnants of the civilizations remain a draw for curious scholars and greedy treasure hunters willing to brave the cruel desert in search of what these cultures left behind.

This document provides content to help you run games that feature deserts of various types, from rocky wastelands to vast seas of sand as far as the eye can see. This content ranges from character options for players who want their characters to be associated with the harsh wilderness of the desert to mechanics for running sessions with travel through deserts to a bestiary of desert creatures to populate the arid regions of your games. Whether the players are only spending a day traveling through these regions or your entire campaign takes place in a desert setting, any of the content here can be used to enhance your games.

## Desert Adventure Ideas

Deserts make for harsh adventure locales where the environment can be a greater threat than any creature the players face. The following adventure hooks can be used as inspiration for creating your own adventures in these unforgiving wastes. They could either be minor side quests or part of much larger campaign-spanning adventures.

##### Desert Adventure Hooks

| d8  | Setup |
|:---:|:-----------:|
|  1  | **The Desert Caravan:**  A trade caravan is preparing for the arduous journey through an unforgiving desert filled with predators and bandits who lie in wait along the trade route. The adventurers have been offered the chance to travel with the caravan and use their supplies in exchange for protection.  |
|  2  | **Not a Drop To Drink:** An oasis sustaining a desert settlement is running dry, and the adventurers have been tasked with discovering the source of the low water supply or finding a new supply before it's too late. |
|  3  | **Blast Zone:** An arcane disaster has left an entire region a desolate, arid wasteland wracked by magical anomalies. The adventurers have been tasked with exploring this perilous region now populated by eldritch mutants and horrors and determining the cause of the disaster and whether the effects can be reversed.  |

\columnbreak

<br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br>

|   |  |
|:---:|:-----------:|
|  4  | **The Lost Tomb:** The adventurers have been hired by unscrupulous treasure hunters to help find an ancient tomb thought lost in the desert wastes. Even if they find the tomb, can they trust the treasure hunters to make good on their promises when they get there?  |
|  5  | **Left for Dead:** After being captured by a brutal foe, the adventurers are left for dead in the desert. Now they must use their wits to survive and escape back to civilization and settle the score.   |
|  6  | **The Lost Archaeologists:** Several scholars traveled into the desert in search of ancient ruins and haven't been heard from since. The adventurers have been tasked with following their trail and determining their fate.  |
|  7  | **The Desert's Spread:** A group of druids tasked with maintaining a desert border have found themselves unable to stop the desert's spread as its sands slowly engulf the verdant neighboring region. The druids have enlisted the aid of the adventurers in finding the cause of this spread. |
|  8  | **The Sandstorm:** The adventurers find themselves in a small desert settlement preparing to weather an incoming sandstorm. As the storm approaches, they learn of its unnatural size and the horrors lurking within.  |

## Druid Circle

### Circle of the Desert
The druids of this circle hail from some of the driest and most
unforgiving environments imaginable. Yet, even in these deadly
realms, life can thrive, and these druids seek to emulate the
tenacity of the desert's inhabitants while also channeling its
brutality against nature's enemies. To this end, these druids
can shapeshift into some of the desert's most deadly creatures
while also calling upon the scorching sand and heat of their
homeland to rapidly wither away their foes.

#### Desert Spells
*2nd-Level Circle of the Desert Feature*

Your mystical connection to the desert infuses you with the
ability to cast certain spells. Once you gain access to one of
these spells, you always have it prepared, and it doesn't count
against the number of spells you can prepare each day. If you
gain access to a spell that doesn't appear on the Druid spell
list, the spell is nonetheless a Druid spell for you. (New spells and revised spells can be found at end of document.)

##### Circle of the Desert Spells

| Druid Level | Spells |
|:-----------:|:------:|
| 2nd | *Warding Light*, *Sand Blast** |
| 3rd | *Dust Devil**, *Ray of Enfeeblement* |
| 5th | *Wall of Sand, Dazzling Light* |
| 7th | *Decrepify**, *Sickening Radiance* |

#### Desert Forms
*2nd-Level Circle of the Desert Feature*

You can use your Wild Shape feature to transform into unique
desert animal forms granted by this subclass. When using your
Wild Shape this way, you can use it as a Bonus Action. Detailed
descriptions of these forms can be found in the Desert Wild
Shapes section.

When shapeshifting into one of these forms, you can choose to
be Small or Medium. Starting at 10th level, you can also choose
to be Large.

While in these forms, you can perform the Somatic and Verbal
components of Druid spells from your Desert Spells list and
ignore their Material component requirements.

You begin knowing three of these forms: Camel, Canid, and
Venomous Snake. Additional forms become available at levels 4
and 8.

#### Arid Adaptations
*2nd-Level Circle of the Desert Feature*

You gain Resistance to Fire damage. If you already have
Resistance to Fire damage from another source, you instead have
Advantage on Dexterity saving throws against effects that
deal Fire damage to you. You also require only half as much
water each day to avoid the effects of dehydration, and your
movement is unaffected by difficult terrain composed of sand.


Additionally, you have Advantage on Wisdom (Survival) and
Intelligence (Nature) checks relating to desert environments
and the creatures that inhabit them. You can also speak with
animals that dwell in desert environments as though you were
under the effects of the *Speak with Animals* spell, and you
have Advantage on Wisdom (Animal Handling) checks made while
interacting with such creatures.

#### Primal Weapons
*6th-Level Circle of the Desert Feature*

When you merge into one of your Wild Shape forms while wielding
a magical weapon, your attacks in that form count as magical
for the purpose of overcoming Resistance and Immunity to
nonmagical attacks and damage.

#### Heat of the Desert
*6th-Level Circle of the Desert Feature*

You can call forth the punishing heat of your homeland. As an Action, you conjure a 20-foot-radius Sphere of withering heat centered on a point you can see within 30 feet of yourself.

Whenever a creature of your choice within the area makes a Strength, Dexterity, or Constitution-based D20 Test, it takes 1d6 Fire damage. This damage is dealt after the result of the test is determined. If the creature fails the test, it takes 2d6 Fire damage instead.

As a Bonus Action, you can move the Sphere up to 20 feet. The Sphere lasts for 1 minute, or until you dismiss it (no action required).

Once you use this feature, you can't do so again until you finish a Long Rest, unless you expend a spell slot of 3rd level or higher to use it again.

\columnbreak

#### Avatar of the Desert
*10th-Level Circle of the Desert Feature*

When you use your Wild Shape feature to transform into one of
the forms granted by this subclass, you can choose one of the
following enhancements:

- **Sand Form.** Your form is composed of shifting sand. You along with any equipment you are wearing or carrying can move through any space as narrow as 1 inch without squeezing ignore, and you can move through enemies without having your movement speed slowed, you ignore difficult terrain case by any ground, you can't be grappled or restrained. Your attacks also gain additional reach of 10 feet.

- **Mirage Form.** Your form takes on a shimmering, illusory
quality. While in this form, you are under the effects of
the *Mirror Image* spell.

- **Mummy Form.** Your form becomes horrifically desiccated,
infused with necrotic energy. When you deal none bludgeoning, piercing, or slashing damage with a Circle of the Desert Spells, your Heat of the Desert feature, or your wilde Shape, you deal necrotic damage instead. If that creature
then has a number hit points remaining lower then your druid level, it is reduced to 0
hit points and turns to dust.

#### Withering Heat
*14th-Level Circle of the Desert Feature*

You can cast *Withering Heat** without expending a spell slot,
and it doesn't require Concentration for you. It counts as a
Druid spell for you, and you can cast it while in one of your
Desert Forms as though it were one of your Desert Spells.

Once you cast the spell this way, you can't do so again until
you finish a Long Rest, unless you expend a spell slot of 7th
level or higher.

> ##### Alternative Condition: Fatigue
> The creature experiences debilitating weariness that can accumulate in levels. Each time a creature gains the Fatigue condition, its Fatigue Level increases by 1. If the level reaches 6, the creature dies. Fatigue Levels have the following effects on a creature:

# Desert Travel
Deserts are among the cruelest environments for overland travel. They are usually sparsely inhabited and rarely offer marked paths for travellers to follow. Even when following a marked route, their lack of resources and temperature extremes make any journey deadly without heavy provisions and proper protective gear. Those who become lost in the desert quickly burn through their water rations and are left to wither away in a near-lifeless wasteland. 

## Travel Activities
When the players travel long distances overland, desert environments can be a deadly challenge in their journey. You may modify the overland travel activities detailed in the *Player's Handbook* in the following ways when the players travel through such terrain.

### Travel Pace
If the players are traveling over deep sand dunes or across very rough and rocky land features, it is considered rough terrain unless. Most normal mounts and vehicles will also have trouble moving through snowy terrain, and the players may need to use creatures such as camels to move across heavy sand.

\columnbreak

### Navigation
The difficulty in navigating desert environments can vary greatly. The DMG suggests a navigation DC of around 10 for most desert environments. However, expansive, featureless sandscapes would likely have a DC of 15.

If most of the environment's land features are defined by sand dunes, high winds (or the rare flash flood) can cause major shifts in the landscape, distorting any prior familiarity the players have with an area.

However, the lack of cloud cover means players traveling at night can use the stars to orient themselves, negating any disadvantage that would normally be applied from navigating the dark. 

### Foraging
Desert environments tend to have little to no natural resources. In most deserts, the foraging DC should be 20 or higher, with the only exceptions being areas near a river or oasis where the DC can be as low as 10.


### Taking Shelter
While any environment can pose challenges to travelers looking to make shelter, deserts are particularly unforgiving. Travellers often rest during midday to avoid the deadly desert heat, and tents will usually be needed to keep sand and sun at bay. If a particularly fierce windstorm or other weather event picks up, most travellers are dangerously exposed unless they can find rocky outcroppings to hide among.

## Desert Hazards
Deserts are among the most cruel and deadly natural environments. Extreme cold, extreme heat, strong wind, and quicksand are all common desert hazards described in the DMG. Other hazards include sandstorms, mirages, and even the rare flash flood. New and alternative rules for how to run these hazards are described below. 

### Flash Floods
Flash floods are sudden, rapid flooding events caused by intense rainfall, dam breaks, or other sudden water releases. They can occur with little warning and have devastating effects on both the landscape and any creatures caught in their path.

#### Noticing Floods
Players can generally notice the onset of a flash flood when it's 3d6 x 10 ft. away. The DC to spot the flood starts at 12 and decreases by 1 for every 10 ft. closer they get.

#### Getting Caught in a Flood
Being caught in a flash flood can be extremely dangerous, presenting multiple hazards to creatures in the area.

##### Initial Impact
When a creature enters the area of the flood, it must make a Strength saving throw. On a failed save, the creature takes the flood's full amount of bludgeoning damage. On a successful save, it takes half as much damage.

\columnbreak

##### Movement and Further Impact
At the start of each turn, the leading edge of the flood, along with any creatures in it, moves 50 feet downhill. Any Huge or smaller creature inside the flood when it moves must succeed on a Strength saving throw or take half the flood's bludgeoning damage. A creature can take this damage only once per round.

##### Swimming in the Flood
A creature caught in the flood can move by swimming. Because of the force of the water, though, the creature must make a successful Strength (Athletics) check against the flood's save DC in order to move at all. If it fails the check, it can’t move.

##### Flood Severity Table
Use the table below to determine the severity of the flood, the saving throw DC, and the bludgeoning damage:

| d6 | Flood Size | Flood Depth (ft.) | Flood Save DC | Flood Damage |
|:--:|:---------:|:-----------------:|:-------------:|:-------------:|
| 1  | Minor     | 1d4               | 10            | 2             |
| 2  | Small     | 2d4               | 12            | 2d4           |
| 3  | Moderate  | 2d6               | 15            | 2d6           |
| 4  | Large     | 4d6               | 17            | 4d6           |
| 5  | Massive   | 4d10              | 20            | 4d10          |
| 6  | Extreme   | 5d12              | 25            | 5d12          |

> ##### But I'm Taller Than The Flood!
> For tall creatures in small floods, it may be easier to resist the being swept away. You can use the following rule of thumb for these situations (assuming the creature isn't prone): 
> 
> If a creature is at least twice as tall as the flood's depth, it has advantage on saves against the flood, and if the creature is at least four times as tall as the flood's depth, it automatically passes saves against the flood.

### Mirages
During the day, mirages can be mistaken for land features that players are on the lookout for. Mirages can be seen for the illusions they really are with a DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check. Creatures with proficiency with Survival have an advantage on this check. On a failure, the creature observing the mirage believes it to be a real land feature off in the distance. If the creature tries to travel to this illusory land feature, it will automatically become lost as the feature seemingly disappears.

### Quicksand 
Swampy and sandy environments can form quicksand, which can quickly trap those who stumble within. The DMG already contains rules for including quicksand in your adventures. The following are additional tools you can use to spot and escape quicksand. To randomly determine the size of a pool of quicksand, you can make it 3d6 ft. deep with a 3d6 ft. diameter. Otherwise, you can pick the depth that best suits the danger you want to create.

#### Spotting Quicksand
Rather than using passive Perception to spot quicksand, you may opt to use the passive Survival of any characters keeping a lookout. The DC to spot the quicksand can be anywhere from 10 (an obvious pool) to 30 (supernaturally hidden quicksand). You can pick what's appropriate for your environment or roll to determine one randomly.

\columnbreak

#### Escaping Quicksand (Alternative Method)
The rules for escaping quicksand in the DMG involve using a Strength check. However, quicksand can usually be escaped more reliably by moving in such a way to break the suction that's pulling the creature down, thus allowing the creature to float to the top and paddle out.

**Paddling Out of Quicksand.** A creature trapped in quicksand can use its action to make a DC 15 Dexterity check to attempt to break the suction holding it in the sand. It has an advantage in this check if it falls prone onto its back. On a success, it is no longer Restrained, and can swim out of the quicksand as though it were rough terrain.

<br>

> ##### Realistic vs. Cinematic Quicksand
> In fiction, quicksand will suck adventurers to their doom, leaving them to suffocate in their viscous depths. In reality, people usually only sink to their waists in quicksand because it's denser than a person and thus can be floated on. How you choose to use quicksand in your adventures is up to you.

### Tornadoes

Tornadoes are violent windstorms characterized by a twisting, funnel-shaped cloud. They can cause severe damage to the environment and present significant dangers to any creatures caught in their path. Tornadoes can form quickly and are often unpredictable in their movement and intensity.

#### Spotting Tornadoes
Spotting tornadoes can be challenging due to various factors, including weather conditions and visibility. DMs will need to make their own judgment call on the DC to spot tornadoes. Those with proficiency in Nature may have advantage on checks to spot them.

#### Size
Tornadoes vary wildly in size and shape. Their height will always stretch up into clouds, but if you want to randomly determine a tornado's diameter, you can use the following table:

##### Tornado Diameter

| 1-2  | 3d6                |
| 3-5  | 3d6 x 5            |
| 6-8  | 3d6 x 10           |
| 9-13   | 3d6 x 50           |
| 14-17   | 3d6 x 100          |
| 18-19| 3d6 x 500          |
| 20    | 3d6 x 1000         |

#### Effects Near a Tornado
The area near a tornado is extremely dangerous due to high winds and flying debris. The size of this Danger Zone around the tornado can vary depending on its size and severity, as shown in the Tornado Severity table. 

##### High Winds 
Use the rules for high winds described in the DMG for a guideline on how to handle their effect in the danger zone. 

##### Flying Debris
While in the danger zone, players are in danger of getting hit by flying debris. To randomly determine if a piece of debris is flying at a creature, roll a d10 at the start of each of its turns. If the result is less than or equal to the Wind Severity level, the player must succeed on a Dexterity save against the tornado's save DC or take bludgeoning damage equal to the tornado's debris damage. You can increase or decrease the size of the die used to account for tornadoes with more or less debris flying about.

\columnbreak

#### Getting Caught in a Tornado
Though tornadoes can't pull creatures into their vortex, any creature that is blown into the funnel is likely to suffer catastrophic effects.

##### Initial Contact
A creature that comes in contact with a tornado must make a Strength save against the tornado's save DC, with the following potential outcomes:
* **Pass By 5 Or More:** No effect.
* **Pass By Less Than 5:** The creature is pushed by half the tornado's throw distance in a random direction.
* **Fail By Less Than 5:** The creature is thrown the tornado's throw distance a random direction.
* **Fail By 5 Or More:** The creature gets picked up by the tornado. (Tornadoes with a Severity Level of 1 can't pick up creatures)

##### Being Picked Up by a Tornado
Once a creature is picked up by a tornado, it remains carried by the wind for a number of rounds determined by the tornado's severity. The creature must make a Dexterity save every turn to avoid debris, as described in the Flying Debris section. After the time in the tornado has expired, the creature is thrown the tornado's Throw Distance from a height of 1d6 x 100 ft. per round spent in the tornado.

### Winds
Rules for strong winds are described on page 110 of the DMG. If you wish to make your winds particularly hazardous, you can modify them in the following ways; the save DCs and severity of the effects these winds have are determined by the Wind Severity table.

##### Damaging Winds
Some winds are deadly for reasons beyond their sheer force. They may carry extreme cold or fine debris that can slash anything caught in their path. For every 1 minute creatures spend in such wind, they must make a Constitution save (DC dependent on wind severity). On a failure, the creature takes an amount of damage dependent on the severity of the wind, and on a pass, it takes half as much damage.

**Extreme Cold.** This wind carries a chill that cuts to the core. It deals cold damage to those caught in its effects. Creatures with cold resistance can ignore its effects, and creatures with insulating fur or clothing have advantage on saves against it.

**Sand & Ice Storms.** This wind carries many fine particles that shred everything in its path. It deals slashing damage to those caught in its effects, with the amount of damage varying based not only on how high the wind speeds are, but also on how many of these particles are present in the wind. Light amounts of these particles create lightly obscured areas, while heavy amounts create heavily obscured areas. Creatures with heavy armor and face protections or other forms of full body protection have advantage on saves against it.

\columnbreak

<br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br>



##### Sudden Gusts
This wind will unexpectedly pick up gusts capable of bowling over creatures. At the start of every round of combat with this wind, roll 1d6. On a result of 5 or 6, all creatures not behind at least half cover are subjected to the effects of the *gust of wind* spell, except the distance they are moved on a failed save is dependent on the severity of the wind. 

If the players aren't in combat, this wind will greatly hamper travel for those not behind cover and make traveling near crevasses very dangerous. If players are traveling for an extended period of time through an open area subjected to sudden gusts, have each of them make a Strength save for each hour of travel. On a success, they are able to push through the winds, and on a failure, they treat the terrain areas as though it's rough terrain while they struggle to fight against the winds. If the terrain is already rough terrain, creatures can only move through it at 1/4th the speed.

## Armadillos
*Adaptable Mammalian Tanks of The Animal Kingdom*<br>
**Habitat:** Desert, Forest, Grasslands, Hills, Swamps; <br> **Treasure:** None <br>
Armadillos are among the strangest of mammals. These small burrowing creatures possess a hardened shell across their backs, segmented with leathery bands that grant them remarkable flexibility compared to other armored animals. The three-banded armadillos are even capable of short bursts of speed to escape predators. If such sprints fail, they can roll into a tight ball, fully enclosing themselves so that their bony armor protects their entire body.

Many subterranean races keep armadillos as pets. Some powerful celestials who command warlocks even grant these humble creatures as familiars to their devoted servants, for armadillos embody both protection and humility.


### Armadillo Tactics
Armadillos are timid and avoid combat whenever possible, fleeing or burrowing to escape danger.

As familiars, they use the Help action, then roll into a defensive ball to protect themselves.

### Hunter Armadillos

Hunter armadillos, often called Killer Dillos, are far more fearsome than their tiny kin. Roughly the size of a small bear, they possess massive claws capable of tearing through earth and powerful jaws lined with sharp teeth that can seize and hold prey in place.

These predators hunt small subterranean creatures such as gophers and snakes, as well as small humanoids like goblins and kobolds. They can even be found stalking prey in the Underdark, where they are capable of short, explosive sprints to close the distance.

If gravely wounded, a hunter armadillo may curl into a defensive ball like its smaller kin, though its sheer size makes this a last resort rather than a reliable defense.

### Hunter Armadillo Tactics
Hunter armadillos Dash to engage, attempting to grapple and drag targets away from allies.

If overwhelmed, they roll into a defensive ball until they can reengage or escape.

When directed by intelligent allies, they rush the back line to disrupt weaker targets. Enemies must choose between wasting time taking out the armadillos, who will roll into a ball, or ignoring them and suffering continued attacks.

## Armadillofolk
*Hardened Nomads United Through Sacred Familial Bonds*<br>
**Habitat:** Desert, Forest, Grasslands, Hills, Swamps; **Treasure:** Relics <br>


Within tunnels carved into the hillsides of arid landscapes dwell a people who have endured the test of time again and again. The armadillofolk hold no value more sacred than the bond and defense of one’s siblings.

For these armored folk, this bond is inherent, as armadillofolk mothers always give birth to identical quadruplets of the same sex. Stranger still, these siblings share a connection known simply as “the Roll.” This bond psychically links the four together, allowing them to share thoughts and emotions. The psionic link strengthens as they age, enabling flawless coordination without a word spoken. With discipline, this power can even be used to subtly manipulate their own bodies.


***Union of Bonds.*** Armadillofolk society is entirely structured around the value of one’s Roll. A set of quadruplets never willingly separates and makes all major decisions collectively.

\columnbreak

<br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br>

Upon reaching adulthood, a Roll typically sets out into a nomadic life, relying solely on one another for survival. In time, female Rolls may settle if they discover a suitable place to carve a lasting settlement into the earth. Male Rolls continue wandering until they encounter a group of unattached female Rolls; at that point, both Rolls may consider union.

When determining such unions, individual attraction carries little weight. What matters is the compatibility of the two Rolls as unified entities. If the pairing is agreed upon, one male and one female from each Roll will mate, producing a new set of quadruplets.

***Shells and Loyalty of Stone.*** The armadillofolk worship no gods. It is said among them that they were created to mock and defy the gods themselves. The details of this ancient humiliation vary from Roll to Roll, each preserving its own version of which deities were mocked and how.

Having forsaken divine worship, their spiritual practices instead center upon the veneration of the Roll. Rituals are performed to strengthen familial bonds. Some take the form of prolonged meditations within special chambers, lasting for days, during which the minds of the four interlock so completely that the sense of individual self nearly dissolves. Others involve the creation of sacred artifacts: each member contributes to the crafting and psychically imprints upon the object as it is formed. Weaving agave fibers and shaping jade are among the primary crafts of these rites.

So sacred is the Roll that an armadillofolk individual will sacrifice anything to protect their brothers or sisters. They will endure, or even commit, grievous acts if it ensures the safety of their siblings.


***Battle Brothers and Sisters.*** Armadillofolk are generally peaceful and avoid confrontation, as well as prolonged relationships with other peoples, save for trading the gems they mine and the garments they weave.

However, should one member of a Roll be threatened, the aggressor must contend with the entire quartet until driven off or slain.

In battle, armadillofolk fight with deadly harmony. They execute precise maneuvers without speech, wielding jade weapons infused with psionic force and crafted by their own Roll. They can curl into armored spheres, deflecting lethal blows from their hardened shells. Using telekinetic force, they launch themselves forward, barreling into foes with devastating impact.

Even aerial opponents are not safe from these tactics. A Roll of armadillofolk can transform into a barrage of living missiles in an instant.

***Found Family.*** It is possible, through powerful ritual and the expenditure of great quantities of jade, for an armadillofolk individual to form a Roll with non-sibling creatures. Such an act is deeply controversial. Most armadillofolk consider bonding outside one’s birth Roll unnatural, even blasphemous. Those who attempt it usually do so out of desperation.

### Armadillofolk Tactics
Armadillofolk prioritize disrupting vulnerable backline targets, using javelins to pressure spellcasters and ranged enemies. If all four members of a Roll can coordinate, they will use Roll Out on a single target to quickly overwhelm it. This tactic is risky, as emerging from their shells costs a full action.

If a member becomes bloodied or overwhelmed, it will use Roll Out defensively to disengage and escape. Even while rolled up, armadillofolk remain useful by positioning adjacent to enemies to grant advantage to their allies’ attacks.

## Camels
*Paragons of Endurance & Survival In The Harsh Desert*<br>
**Habitat:** Desert; **Treasure:** None <br>
Camels are one of the few large animals able to thrive in the harshest deserts of the world. Whether in biting cold or sweltering heat, these creatures can be found in herds striding for miles across the sand in both extremes of temperature, able to go without water or food for days. Though camels are protected by the mere fact that most predators cannot survive in such brutal wastelands, they are not defenseless. Despite their lack of tusks, horns, or even hard hooves, a camel’s bite is particularly brutal for a herbivore and is capable of breaking bones.

Such an ability to withstand hardship has been harnessed by many peoples, allowing them to traverse vast dunes with all manner of goods secured to the backs of these animals. Even in warfare, many armies have used the hump-backed beast as a mount, replacing horses in such regions. So necessary are camels that they have become symbols of civilization and survival.

## Giant Sun Spiders
*Fast & Deadly Arachnid Hunters of The Desert*<br>
**Habitat:** Desert; **Treasure:** None <br>
Sand is flung into the air by hair-covered legs as they scuttle in unison across the dry dunes at speeds that make them appear as little more than a blur to any observer. These limbs are attached to a large, oval body crowned by a massive head, its chelicerae chittering ominously. Such a sight can mean only one thing: the approach of the beast known as a Wind Scorpion, or Sun Spider.

Though they belong to neither the spider nor scorpion families, they are their own distinct type of arachnid. Stalking deserts and barren, flat landscapes, they hunt in the dead of night, sprinting across the earth at speeds rivaling a galloping horse, guided by their pedipalps and the faintest vibrations as they navigate the darkness. With such agility, they bear down on prey with chitinous maws as sharp as greatswords, slicing hunks from their victims and leaving behind gaping wounds.

Such a creature is nearly untamable. Its speed and rapacious appetite drive it to tear apart anything within reach. To slay such a nightmare is considered a great feat among most cultures, for only the most skilled warriors dare face these skittering terrors, and even the finest armor offers little protection against the remorseless weapons of these predators.

## Scorpions

**Habitat:** Desert, Forest, Grasslands, Hills; <br> **Treasure:** None <br>
Few creatures inspire the same instinctive dread as the scorpion. Found lurking beneath rocks, within desert sands, and inside the boots of travelers who failed to check before dressing, these armored arachnids are among the most ancient and adaptable predators in the natural world. ***Ambush Predators.*** Scorpions are not pursuit hunters. They are patient, opportunistic, and disinclined to expend energy they don't need to. A scorpion will spend the better part of a day motionless beneath a rock or buried just below the surface of the sand, waiting for vibrations in the ground to signal the approach of something worth eating. When prey draws close enough, the scorpion moves with surprising speed, seizing it in its claws before driving its stinger home.

&emsp;***Venom and Armor.*** The scorpion's exoskeleton is a formidable piece of natural engineering, providing protection that belies the creature's small frame. Coupled with its venom, which varies dramatically in potency from species to species, the scorpion is equipped both to avoid becoming a meal and to secure one. Some scorpion venoms produce little more than localized pain, while others are capable of inducing paralysis, organ failure, or death in creatures far larger than the scorpion itself. Alchemists and poison-crafters prize scorpion venom highly, and the dangerous business of harvesting it from living specimens is a trade practiced in regions where scorpions are plentiful and coin is scarce.

***Desert Omens.*** In many cultures that make their homes in arid and desert environments, scorpions carry significant symbolic weight. They are seen variously as emblems of resilience, harbingers of death, or sacred creatures associated with gods of the underworld and poison. It is not uncommon for desert-dwelling peoples to incorporate scorpion imagery into their warriors' arms and armor, believing it to confer some measure of the creature's hardiness and lethality upon the wearer. Some nomadic tribes regard the unexpected appearance of a scorpion as a warning, and will break camp and move on rather than risk whatever ill fortune the creature's presence is thought to foretell.

***Deathstalker Scorpions.*** Despite its fearsome name, the deathstalker is a relatively slender and delicate-looking creature compared to many of its bulkier cousins. Its thin claws are weak enough that it cannot rely on them to restrain prey, which has driven the development of its primary asset: a venom of extraordinary and disproportionate potency. A deathstalker's sting has the potential to kill an adult humanoid, and smaller or more vulnerable creatures stand little chance of surviving an encounter with one unassisted. Paradoxically, the deathstalker's venom has also drawn the attention of healers and alchemists, who have found that certain compounds within it, carefully extracted and prepared, show remarkable medicinal properties. This combination of lethal danger and potential curative value makes the deathstalker one of the most sought-after scorpions in the world, and the specialists who make their living capturing them alive command steep prices for their services.

***Scorpion Swarms.*** A single scorpion is an <br> unpleasant encounter. A swarm of them is a <br> nightmare. When driven from their burrows by <br> flooding, magical disturbance, or simply the <br> malicious intent of someone who knows what <br> they are doing, scorpions will surge across the <br> ground in a living tide of claws and stingers. <br> Travelers caught in the open by such a <br> swarm face a relentless barrage of stings <br> from every angle, with no single point of <br> attack to defend against. Those who<br> survive the experience rarely forget the <br> sound: a dry, chittering rustle that <br> precedes the swarm like a whispered <br> warning. A deathstalker swarm is <br> considerably rarer and considerably <br> more lethal, and the mere rumor of <br> one in a region is typically enough <br> to empty a village overnight.

Master and subordinate share a single purpose: to spread the seeds nestled within their brittle forms across the wasteland, ensuring the next generation may take root. The elemental guides and propels its charges, ushering the reanimated tumbleweeds across the land in search of ideal places to disperse their young. It also serves as their guardian, defending the caravan from any who would attempt to halt its pilgrimage.

These plants do not seek to harm other living things. They bear no malice, no quarrel with the world around them. Yet their relentless mission makes them dangerous. Anything that impedes their path risks being swept aside without hesitation. Tumbleweed Wranglers have been known to cross farmland, pulverizing crops and sundering fences as they pass.

Their dry forms are highly flammable, making them severe fire hazards, capable of spreading flames rapidly if ignited. Stopping such a caravan is no simple task. The guiding elemental can unleash powerful gales, flinging foes aside, and summon torrents of crackling energy to electrocute its enemies. At its command, the rolling masses of barbed husks may surge forward, driven by violent winds, their hardened spines slamming into adversaries and tearing into flesh.

If truly desperate, the elemental may even ignite its own undead charges, sacrificing them in a blazing onslaught if it believes it necessary to preserve the greater swarm.

### Tumbleweed Wrangler Tactics
The wrangler’s primary strategy is to use Rushing Gust to push as many reanimated tumbleweeds into enemies as possible. Positioning is key, and the battlefield can be manipulated in a variety of ways. Keep the following in mind:

**Rushing Gust (Core Strategy):**
Aim to line up tumbleweeds and opponents so a single use of Rushing Gust causes multiple collisions. Think in terms of angles and chain reactions rather than single targets.
 
**Air Lasso (Setup Tool):**
Use Air Lasso to hurl a tumbleweed into an enemy, then position that tumbleweed so a later Rushing Gust will push it into another target.

Alternatively, target a low-Strength opponent: pull them out of position and fling them into a tumbleweed. When you follow up with Rushing Gust, that enemy can be knocked into additional tumbleweeds for extra impact.

> ##### New Condition: Burning
> The creature has been set ablaze by something that continues to damage it each round until it is extinguished. The default source described here is fire, but other sources could deal different types of damage, such as acid.
> * **Default Damage:** A burning creature takes 1d4 fire damage at the end of each of its turns unless the source that inflicted the condition states otherwise.
> * **Ending the Condition:** As an action, the burning creature can extinguish the fire by dropping prone and rolling on the ground. The fire also goes out if it is doused, submerged, or suffocated. For example, another creature could use an action to put out the flames with their cloak or by pouring water on them.
> * **Multiple Sources of Burning:** If a burning creature would gain this condition again from another source, the new burning effect only replaces the old if it deals more damage each round. Otherwise, it is ignored.
